6  Evaluating undirected cell-type networks

There is no complete ground-truth GRN for murine gastruloids. Mouse TF catalogues are incomplete, and undirected GGM edges are not the same objects as directed TF–target arrows. Evaluation in the core is therefore relative: methods against each other, against technical resampling of this atlas, and against partial literature priors. It is not a claim of causal recovery.

The score families and stability scenarios below are inspired by GRETA (Badia-i-Mompel et al. 2025) and BEELINE (Pratapa et al. 2020). We do not import those pipelines. We keep three undirected, observational families:

  1. Statistical robustness and distributional alignment — does the fitted Gaussian (or its graph) stay put under resampling, and does it match the type-level law that DeCovarT needs?
  2. Topological comparison — skeleton similarity among the three core estimators, without a true DAG.
  3. Knowledge-driven scoring — TFs connected to plausible targets and pathway overlap, always as overlap with an incomplete prior.

Directed DAGs, multimodal ATAC/ChIP recovery, perturbation forecasting, and any metric that needs a known true skeleton are perspectives (Section 6.6). They judge a different estimand than Equation 4.1.

6.1 Statistical robustness and alignment

These scores do not need a true graph. They ask whether (\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}) is a stable, distinguishable plug-in for Equation 4.2.

6.1.1 Resampling

With one experiment, the honest uncertainty is conditional on this atlas:

  • Technical cell bootstrap within type, labels fixed (Section 5.4). Re-estimate \boldsymbol{\Omega}_{j}; record edge inclusion frequencies (stability selection in the sense of Meinshausen and Bühlmann (Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2010)) and the Frobenius / Bures spread of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}.
  • Gene or cell downsampling as a coverage check. GRETA found that TF and gene sets can look stable while edges collapse under downsampling — a warning we should expect for glasso supports as well.

The three core estimators (huge, SILGGM D-S_GL, PLNnetwork) are deterministic given the data and the penalty. We do not report seed-overlap diagnostics for them.

Biological (multi-organoid) bootstrap is unavailable. Reporting a bootstrap interval as if it were between-experiment uncertainty would repeat the pseudoreplication error of Note 3.4.

Observation-model contamination (wrong sampling layer, spillover, missing types) is not a GRN-stability experiment. It belongs to the misspecification sweep of Section 10.1.

Note 6.1: ℹ️ StARS selects \lambda from edge instability

In huge, StARS (stability approach to regularisation selection) (Liu et al. 2010) does not score likelihood or the numerical accuracy of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}. Liu, Roeder and Wasserman choose the least amount of regularisation that still makes the graph sparse and replicable under random sampling. For each penalty \lambda, the data are subsampled, the graph is re-estimated, and \hat\pi_{gg'}(\lambda) is the fraction of subsamples in which edge \{g,g'\} appears. Edge instability is of the form

2\hat\pi_{gg'}(\lambda)\bigl(1-\hat\pi_{gg'}(\lambda)\bigr),

which is near zero when the edge is almost always absent or almost always present, and largest when it appears about half the time. These terms are averaged over possible edges. StARS then takes the least sparse graph whose overall instability stays below stars.thresh (huge.stars; huge.select).

Practical settings:

  • stars.thresh = 0.1 — package default; allows about 10% graph variability and tends to give a denser network;
  • stars.thresh = 0.05 — stricter, typically sparser; the documentation presents 0.05 as more appropriate when the graphical model is believed to be well specified.

The selected \lambda determines both the adjacency (refit) and the precision (opt.icov). There is no separate StARS criterion for a “best precision matrix”. The huge documentation also warns that StARS can overselect edges in Gaussian graphical models, so we compare it with EBIC (Section 4.4.1) rather than treating it as ground truth. For SILGGM and PLNnetwork we reuse the same instability diagnostic on technical subsamples; we do not pretend those packages ship StARS.

6.1.2 Likelihood and information criteria

On the Gaussian scale actually used by the estimator,

  • eBIC (extended BIC) is the usual glasso selection criterion when FDR methods are not used;
  • held-out log-likelihood on cells left out within type (still technical, not biological).

These scores compare nested Gaussian models. They do not validate a PLN latent graph against a glasso graph on NPN data: the observation models differ. Cross-family comparison must move to a common object — the exported (\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}) on the linear DeCovarT scale — and use distributional distances.

6.1.3 Distances between type-level Gaussians

Closed forms, for non-degenerate P_{j}=\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}):

Kullback–Leibler

\begin{aligned} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{j}\parallel P_{\ell}) &= \tfrac12\Bigl[ \operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\ell}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}) +(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j})^{\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\ell}^{-1} (\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j}) -G +\ln\frac{\det\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\ell}}{\det\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}} \Bigr]. \end{aligned} \tag{6.1}

Jeffreys J(P_{j},P_{\ell})=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{j}\parallel P_{\ell})+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{\ell}\parallel P_{j}) is the symmetric version used as a separation monitor in the feature chapter.

Hellinger via Gaussian affinity, and MixSim overlap \omega_{j\ell}^{\mathrm{MS}} as the sum of misclassification probabilities (Melnykov et al. 2012, 2021), are the right “can DeCovarT tell these types apart?” scores. They are not graph metrics; they are metrics on the generative object. (The superscript \mathrm{MS} avoids clashing with precision entries \omega_{gg'}.)

Bures–Wasserstein (Section 5.7.1) is a perspective metric. It splits mean displacement from covariance geometry when an external population Gaussian is available.

Note 6.2: ℹ️ Compare laws on the DeCovarT scale

KL between NPN-space Gaussians is not KL between count-scale pseudo-bulks. After each estimator has exported Equation 4.9, compute KL / Hellinger / W_{2} / MixSim on that Gaussian, the one that enters the convolution. Otherwise a “better” SILGGM graph can win on NPN likelihood and lose as a deconvolution prior.

6.2 Topological comparison

These describe shape and concordance, not correctness against a true DAG. The core object is the undirected adjacency A_{gg'}=\mathbf{1}\{\hat\omega_{gg'}\neq 0\}.

  • degree distribution and a power-law diagnostic (many biological networks are not strictly scale-free);
  • hub overlap across types (rewiring of hubs is a DeCovarT-relevant summary);
  • clustering coefficient, number of connected components, modularity;
  • overlap coefficients of edge sets between the three methods (intersection over the smaller set), which avoids punishing a method for returning a larger graph.

Structural distances between two estimated undirected graphs (no true skeleton required) follow the bnlearn comparison helpers (Comparing network structures):

  • hamming() — Hamming distance: the number of edge additions and deletions needed to turn one skeleton into the other (no orientation term, because our graphs are undirected);
  • shd() — structural Hamming distance (SHD). For DAGs it also counts reversals; on undirected skeletons it reduces to the same add/delete count. Report both the raw distance and a version normalised by G(G-1)/2.

Concordance between SILGGM and PLNnetwork is a robustness number, not a validation. If the three core estimators disagree on edges, that is the expected state of observational GRN inference, not a bug unique to gastruloids. Downstream DeCovarT should therefore not depend on a single edge being true; it should depend on the SPD covariance being a stable discriminator.

Metrics that need a known true skeleton (precision, recall, F, AUPRC, MCC against a simulator DAG) are in Section 6.6.1.

6.3 Visualisations

The aim is to compare structure and inference, not to decorate a methods section. Each display should answer one question. Penalty-path and funkyheatmap scoreboards live in Section 4.4; this section is about supports, signs, and modules.

Supports and concordance (one type, one time)

  • Binary adjacency heatmaps, genes ordered by a common hierarchical clustering (from a consensus distance d_{ab}=1-\bar r_{ab} of methods, or from a biological ordering). Side-by-side heatmaps of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j} for two types make rewiring visible; a third panel of |\hat\omega^{(j)}_{gg'}-\hat\omega^{(\ell)}_{gg'}| is the differential-network view.
  • Upset plots of edge presence across huge, SILGGM and PLNnetwork at a fixed type \times time: how many edges are unique to one estimator, shared by two, or shared by all three.
  • Upset plots of edge sign, which is stricter. An edge is a signed pair \bigl(\{g,g'\},\operatorname{sign}(\hat\omega_{gg'})\bigr). Two methods can share the support and still disagree on the sign of the partial correlation; that disagreement is visible only in the signed Upset.
  • On-diagonal degree bars or a simple graph drawing only for a tiny TF subgraph; whole-transcriptome hairballs are not figures.

Leiden modules from the precision weights

Treat \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j} as a weighted undirected graph with edge weights |\hat\omega_{gg'}| (or |\rho_{gg'\mid\mathrm{rest}}| from Equation 4.7) on the selected support. Cluster genes with Leiden (igraph::cluster_leiden()): the weights enter the quality function directly, so modules reflect strength of conditional dependence, not just binary adjacency. Compare partitions across the three estimators (adjusted Rand index) and across types (rewiring of modules). Do not feed the dense \check{\boldsymbol{\Omega}} into Leiden.

Signed partial correlations

  • Diverging tiles of -\omega_{gg'}/\sqrt{\omega_{gg}\omega_{g'g'}} with a midpoint scale, not viridis (the latter encodes magnitude, not sign). Keep gene order shared across types.

Stability

  • Edge-inclusion frequencies from the technical bootstrap as a heatmap or as a reliability curve (precision among edges with inclusion >t, as t varies).
  • Sankey or alluvial only when nodes have a natural order (time, lineage). For unordered type-to-type edge flow, a chord diagram is the usual display; we do not need one in the core stationary analysis.

6.4 Knowledge-driven scoring

Literature / knowledge (observational, undirected)

  • Marker TFs: is a known type marker present as a hub, or as a high-degree node in \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j} for that type?
  • TF–target edges: if g is a TF and g' a curated target, does the undirected edge \{g,g'\} appear? Resources: CollecTRI, DoRothEA, and mouse TF catalogues — all incomplete.
  • TF–TF via shared targets: two TFs that share many neighbours may reflect a complex or an artefact of co-expression; report overlap with PPI / IntAct only as a soft score.

Predictive (observational)

  • How well does the graph topology predict held-out expression (gene \sim neighbours)? For a GGM this is almost circular (the model is a prediction of each node given the rest), but a held-out cell nodewise R^{2} is still a useful calibration: a graph that does not predict expression of its own nodes is a bad Gaussian.
  • Gene-set enrichment of Leiden or other modules (Hallmark, KEGG, Reactome) is a sanity check, not a causal test.

6.5 Practical scorecard for this repository

For each core estimator and each cell type j:

  1. Robustness: bootstrap edge-inclusion; StARS-style instability (Note 6.1); W_{2} / KL of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} across bootstraps.
  2. Alignment: KL / Hellinger / MixSim between types (discrimination) and between the type-level Gaussian and the empirical cell cloud (Section 5.1).
  3. Topology: Hamming / SHD and Upset concordance among the three estimators; degree / hub concordance; Leiden ARI; no hairball as a main figure.
  4. Knowledge: fraction of curated TF–target pairs recovered as undirected edges; marker-TF degree; stated as overlap with an incomplete prior, not as accuracy.
  5. Downstream (the score that actually matters for this project): DeCovarT deconvolution error under a fixed mixture design when \boldsymbol{\zeta} comes from each GRN method, with the misspecification sweep of Section 10.1.

Item 5 is the only metric that is identified with the paper’s estimand. Items 1–4 are supporting diagnostics. Perspectives stay out of the scorecard until the corresponding data or simulator is in data/processed/.

6.6 Perspectives

The core graphs are undirected, observational, and fitted without a true gastruloid skeleton. The following tasks are useful later; they are not gates for DeCovarT.

6.6.1 When a true skeleton is available

On a simulator whose GRN we supplied (Section 5.5), or on a curated Boolean skeleton used only as a reference, the usual ranking scores apply to the undirected adjacency:

Score Definition on skeletons Caveat
Precision / recall / F_{1} or F_{0.1} TP, FP, FN against the reference skeleton Needs a true or curated support; GRETA weights precision 10\times recall because inferred GRNs are sparse
Early precision Precision among the top-k edges Robust when k is the expected degree \times G/2
AUPRC / AUROC Ranking of edges AUROC is too optimistic on sparse graphs; prefer AUPRC
Structural Hamming distance Hamming / SHD against the true skeleton Scale with G; report also normalised SHD
MCC Balanced correlation of binary edges Still needs a reference

Without that reference, these numbers are undefined. CPDAG / orientation metrics apply only if we leave the core and score a DAG. We do not, in the main pipeline.

Boolean / logical models (BEELINE curated networks; BoolODE) are mechanistic simulators: truth tables \to ODEs \to synthetic counts. They supply a known skeleton for AUPRC when we simulate. They are not fitted to Suppinger as the DeCovarT graph. Using a Boolean model as the prior and a GGM as the data-driven proposal is a legitimate consistency check; using the Boolean model as \boldsymbol{\Omega}_{j} would abandon the Gaussian convolution.

6.6.2 Directed DAGs

Our estimand is an undirected SPD precision. Directed causal metrics (SHD on orientations, SID, CPDAG comparisons) judge a different object. They belong here only if a later analysis fits a DAG.

6.6.3 Orthogonal assays

ChIP-seq, CUT&RUN, and ATAC-seq (or multiome) networks are closer to physical interaction than a partial correlation. They are orthogonal, complementary modalities; they are not measured in the core Suppinger scRNA-seq table we use for \boldsymbol{\Omega}_{j}. If a later gastruloid multiome appears, GRETA is a possible evaluation harness; SCENIC+ / CellOracle / Dictys remain out of the DeCovarT input, because they return directed regulons, not SPD precisions. The honest use is: project our undirected skeleton onto TF–CRE–gene triples and score literature overlap; do not replace \boldsymbol{\Omega}_{j} with a regulon adjacency.

6.6.4 Perturbations and integer programmes

We do not have a gastruloid TF knockout atlas. GRETA found that multimodal GRNs struggle to capture causal relationships from perturbation assays, even when predictive and literature scores look decent. We therefore do not use TF-activity enrichment after perturbation as a gate for DeCovarT.

CORNETO (Rodriguez-Mier et al. 2025) reformulates knowledge-driven network inference as a joint constrained optimisation problem (LP / MILP) over prior knowledge networks. It is not a GGM. As a perspective scorer, it can take our data-driven support and ask whether a sparse subnetwork of a PKN explains the omics under flow / Steiner / CARNIVAL constraints. That tests mechanistic plausibility, not Gaussian fit.
