6  Evaluating networks without a true DAG

There is no complete ground-truth GRN for murine gastruloids. Mouse TF catalogues are incomplete; ChIP-seq and ATAC-seq are not measured in the core Suppinger scRNA-seq table we use for \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}; and undirected GGM edges are not the same objects as directed TF–target arrows. Evaluation must therefore be relative (methods against each other, against perturbed data, and against partial priors), not a claim of causal recovery.

This chapter groups metrics into three families, following GRETA’s split of mechanistic / predictive / literature tasks and BEELINE’s emphasis on precision–recall against synthetic or curated graphs:

  1. Statistical robustness and distributional alignment — does the fitted Gaussian (or its graph) stay put under resampling, and does it match the type-level law that DeCovarT needs?
  2. Topological comparison — undirected skeleton similarity, without pretending we have orientations.
  3. Knowledge-driven scoring — TFs connected to plausible targets, pathway overlap, integer programmes over prior networks.

Directed causal metrics, multimodal ATAC/ChIP recovery, and perturbation forecasting are perspectives: they judge a different estimand than Equation 4.1.

6.1 What we are not claiming

BEELINE evaluated directed GRN algorithms, often on pseudotime-ordered cells, against synthetic Boolean networks and curated TF–gene graphs. GRETA evaluated multimodal (RNA+ATAC) pipelines on mechanistic perturbation tasks, predictive reconstruction of omics, and literature databases. Our core graphs are undirected, stationary, and transcriptomics-only. We therefore:

  • reduce BEELINE-style scores to skeletons (forget orientation and sign unless a method supplies them);
  • drop GRETA mechanistic forecasting as a core criterion (no perturbation assay on these gastruloids in the core pipeline);
  • keep GRETA-style literature and predictive ideas only where they survive without ATAC.

6.2 Statistical robustness and alignment

These scores do not need a true graph. They ask whether (\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}) is a stable, distinguishable plug-in for Equation 4.2.

6.2.1 Resampling and noise

With one experiment, the honest uncertainty is conditional on this atlas:

  • Technical cell bootstrap within type, labels fixed (Section 5.5). Re-estimate \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}; record edge inclusion frequencies (stability selection in the sense of Meinshausen and Bühlmann (Meinshausen and Bühlmann 2010)) and the Frobenius / Bures spread of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}.
  • Gene or cell downsampling, as GRETA does to test sensitivity to coverage. GRETA found that TF and gene sets can look stable while edges collapse under downsampling — a warning we should expect for glasso supports as well.
  • Random seeds for stochastic estimators (GmGM, Bayesian graphs, boosting). GRETA reported that some multimodal methods reverse high-weight edges across seeds; a GGM that needs a seed should publish edge-overlap across seeds.
  • Observation-model contamination (Gaussian, t_{\nu}, PLN, extra zeros) belongs with Section 8.1, not as a graph-recovery score.

Biological (multi-organoid) bootstrap is unavailable. Reporting a bootstrap interval as if it were between-experiment uncertainty would repeat the pseudoreplication error of Note 3.3.

6.2.2 Likelihood and information criteria

On the Gaussian scale actually used by the estimator,

  • eBIC (extended BIC) is the usual glasso selection criterion when FDR methods are not used;
  • held-out log-likelihood on cells left out within type (still technical, not biological);
  • SILGGM’s own global FDR and, when a simulator supplies a true skeleton, power and Matthews correlation (the package’s simulation diagnostics).

These scores compare nested Gaussian models. They do not validate a PLN latent graph against a glasso graph on NPN data: the observation models differ. Cross-family comparison must move to a common object — the exported (\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}) on the linear DeCovarT scale — and use distributional distances.

6.2.3 Distances between type-level Gaussians

Closed forms, for non-degenerate P_{j}=\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}):

Kullback–Leibler

\begin{aligned} D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{j}\parallel P_{\ell}) &= \tfrac12\Bigl[ \operatorname{tr}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\ell}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}) +(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j})^{\top} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\ell}^{-1} (\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot\ell}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j}) -G +\ln\frac{\det\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\ell}}{\det\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}} \Bigr]. \end{aligned} \tag{6.1}

Jeffreys J(P_{j},P_{\ell})=D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{j}\parallel P_{\ell})+D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{\ell}\parallel P_{j}) is the symmetric version used as a separation monitor in the feature chapter.

Hellinger via Gaussian affinity, and MixSim overlap \omega_{j\ell}=\Omega_{j\ell}+\Omega_{\ell j} as the sum of misclassification probabilities (Melnykov et al. 2012, 2021), are the right “can DeCovarT tell these types apart?” scores. They are not graph metrics; they are metrics on the generative object.

Bures–Wasserstein (Section 5.4) splits mean displacement from covariance geometry. Use it both as a type-separation diagnostic and as an alignment loss between single-cell clouds and type-level Gaussians.

Note 6.1: :information_source: Compare laws on the DeCovarT scale

KL between NPN-space Gaussians is not KL between count-scale pseudo-bulks. After each estimator has exported Equation 4.6, compute KL / Hellinger / W_{2} / MixSim on that Gaussian, the one that enters the convolution. Otherwise a “better” SILGGM graph can win on NPN likelihood and lose as a deconvolution prior.

6.3 Topological comparison

6.3.1 Skeleton scores (BEELINE, reduced to undirected)

BEELINE (Pratapa et al. 2020) is the standard scRNA-seq GRN benchmark. Its headline findings that survive our constraints:

  • AUPRC and early precision are moderate even with known synthetic graphs;
  • methods that do not require pseudotime were generally more accurate — convenient, because we assume stationarity;
  • recovery is easier on simple synthetic networks than on curated Boolean models.

Scores we keep, always on undirected adjacency A_{gg'}=\mathbf{1}\{\hat\theta_{gg'}\neq 0\} (or a thresholded posterior inclusion probability):

Score Definition on skeletons Caveat
Precision / recall / F_{1} or F_{0.1} TP, FP, FN against a reference skeleton Needs a reference; GRETA weights precision 10\times recall because inferred GRNs are sparse
Early precision Precision among the top-k edges Robust when k is the expected degree \times G/2
AUPRC / AUROC Ranking of edges AUROC is too optimistic on sparse graphs; prefer AUPRC
Structural Hamming distance |A-\tilde A| on undirected edges Scale with G; report also normalised SHD
MCC Balanced correlation of binary edges SILGGM simulations use it; still needs a reference

Without a true DAG, the “reference” is one of: (i) a simulator whose GRN we supplied (Section 5.5.3); (ii) another estimator (stability / concordance, not accuracy); (iii) a literature skeleton (Section 6.4). Concordance between SILGGM and PLNnetwork is a robustness number, not a validation.

CPDAG / orientation metrics from the causal-metrics notes apply only if we leave the core and score a DAG. We do not.

6.3.2 Graph summaries that do not need a reference

These describe shape, not correctness:

  • degree distribution and a power-law diagnostic (SILGGM used \log–\log degree plots as a biological sanity check; many biological networks are not strictly scale-free);
  • hub overlap across types (rewiring of hubs is a DeCovarT-relevant summary);
  • clustering coefficient, number of connected components, modularity;
  • overlap coefficients of edge sets between methods, as in GRETA (intersection over the smaller set), which avoids punishing a method for returning a larger graph.

GRETA’s most uncomfortable topological result should be treated as a prior: edge-level overlap across methods is tiny even when TF and gene sets overlap moderately. If our four core estimators disagree on edges, that is the expected state of GRN inference, not a bug unique to gastruloids. Downstream DeCovarT should therefore not depend on a single edge being true; it should depend on the SPD covariance being a stable discriminator.

6.3.3 Visualisations

The aim is to compare structure and inference, not to decorate a methods section. Each display should answer one question.

Supports and concordance

  • Binary adjacency heatmaps, genes ordered by a common hierarchical clustering (from a consensus distance d_{ab}=1-\bar r_{ab} of methods, or from a biological ordering). Side-by-side heatmaps of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{j} for two types make rewiring visible; a third panel of |\hat\theta^{(j)}_{gg'}-\hat\theta^{(\ell)}_{gg'}| is the differential-network view.
  • Upset plots or Jaccard heatmaps of edge sets across the four core methods.
  • On-diagonal degree bars or a simple graph drawing only for a tiny TF subgraph; whole-transcriptome hairballs are not figures.

Signed partial correlations

  • Diverging tiles of partial correlations -\theta_{gg'}/\sqrt{\theta_{gg}\theta_{g'g'}} with a midpoint scale, not viridis (the latter encodes magnitude, not sign). Keep gene order shared across types.

Stability

  • Edge-inclusion frequencies from the technical bootstrap as a heatmap or as a reliability curve (precision among edges with inclusion >t, as t varies).
  • Sankey or alluvial only when nodes have a natural order (time, lineage). For unordered type-to-type edge flow, a chord diagram is the usual display; we do not need one in the core stationary analysis.

Distributional alignment (ties into Section 5.1)

  • PCA / UMAP of cells with the type-level Gaussian as an ellipse is a cartoon (UMAP is not a Gaussian space). Prefer plots of W_{2} or KL against bootstrap replicates, or rainclouds of \hat p from DeCovarT under each GRN method (package helpers plot_mc_raincloud(), plot_mc_forest()), once simulations exist.

GRETA-style genome tracks (TF–CRE–gene) require ATAC and are perspective-only (Section 6.5).

6.4 Knowledge-driven scoring

6.4.1 GRETA’s three buckets, restricted to undirected transcriptomics

GRETA (Badia-i-Mompel et al. 2025) modularises multimodal GRN pipelines and scores them as mechanistic, predictive, and literature-based. Core carry-over:

Literature / knowledge

  • Marker TFs: is a known type marker present as a hub, or as a high-degree node in \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{j} for that type?
  • TF–target edges: if g is a TF and g' a curated target, does the undirected edge \{g,g'\} appear? This is GRETA’s “known interaction” idea with orientation dropped. Resources: CollecTRI, DoRothEA, and mouse TF catalogues — all incomplete.
  • TF–TF via shared targets: two TFs that share many neighbours may reflect a complex or an artefact of co-expression; report overlap with PPI / IntAct only as a soft score.
  • TF-binding / CRE / eQTL recovery: GRETA’s ChIP-Atlas, ReMap, ENCODE, eQTL tasks need sequence assays we do not use in the core.

Predictive (observational)

  • How well does the graph topology predict held-out expression (gene \sim neighbours), as in GRETA’s “Omics” scores? For a GGM this is almost circular (the model is a prediction of each node given the rest), but a held-out cell nodewise R^{2} is still a useful calibration: a graph that does not predict expression of its own nodes is a bad Gaussian.
  • Gene-set enrichment of modules (Hallmark, KEGG, Reactome) is a sanity check, not a causal test.

Mechanistic (perturbation) — perspective. GRETA found that multimodal GRNs struggle to capture causal relationships from perturbation assays, even when predictive and literature scores look decent. That result should humble any claim that our undirected \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} is a regulatory mechanism. We do not have a gastruloid TF knockout atlas in the core pipeline; we therefore do not use TF-activity enrichment after perturbation as a gate for DeCovarT.

GRETA’s other operational warnings that apply unchanged: methods are sensitive to random seeds and to replacing a single pipeline step; paired versus unpaired multiome changes edges even when cell-type abundances look similar; de novo GRN inference from observational data is close to ill-posed. Our four-estimator design is a response to that ill-posedness, not a claim that one method is true.

6.4.2 Integer programmes and Boolean models

CORNETO (Rodriguez-Mier et al. 2025) reformulates knowledge-driven network inference as a joint constrained optimisation problem (LP / MILP) over prior knowledge networks: undirected PPI, signed directed signalling, or metabolic hypergraphs. It is not a GGM. As a perspective scorer, it can take our data-driven support (or a TF–target list derived from it) and ask whether a sparse subnetwork of a PKN explains the omics under flow / Steiner / CARNIVAL constraints. That tests mechanistic plausibility, not Gaussian fit. Joint multi-sample inference is CORNETO’s selling point; with one gastruloid experiment we would be back to a single-sample PKN extract.

Boolean / logical models (the BEELINE curated networks; BoolODE) are simpler mechanistic simulators: truth tables \to ODEs \to synthetic counts. They supply a known skeleton for AUPRC when we simulate (Section 5.5.3). They are not fitted to Suppinger as the DeCovarT graph. Using a Boolean model as the prior and a GGM as the data-driven proposal is a legitimate consistency check; using the Boolean model as \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} would abandon the Gaussian convolution.

6.5 Perspectives: regulatory omics

ChIP-seq, CUT&RUN, and ATAC-seq (or multiome) networks are closer to physical interaction than a partial correlation. GRETA exists because those assays still do not uniquely determine TF–gene edges: CRE-to-gene linking, motif scanning, and the modelling step each change the graph. If a later gastruloid multiome appears, GRETA is the evaluation harness; SCENIC+ / CellOracle / Dictys remain out of the DeCovarT input, because they return directed regulons, not SPD precisions. The honest use is: project our undirected skeleton onto TF–CRE–gene triples and score GRETA literature metrics; do not replace \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} with a regulon adjacency.

6.6 Practical scorecard for this repository

For each core estimator and each cell type j:

  1. Robustness: bootstrap edge-inclusion; W_{2} / KL of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} across bootstraps; seed overlap if stochastic.
  2. Alignment: KL / Hellinger / W_{2} / MixSim between types (discrimination) and between the type-level Gaussian and the empirical cell cloud (Section 5.1).
  3. Topology: pairwise skeleton overlap among the four estimators; degree / hub concordance; no hairball as a main figure.
  4. Knowledge: fraction of curated TF–target pairs recovered as undirected edges; marker-TF degree; stated as overlap with an incomplete prior, not as accuracy.
  5. Downstream (the score that actually matters for this project): DeCovarT deconvolution error under a fixed mixture design when \boldsymbol{\zeta} comes from each GRN method, with the misspecification sweep of Section 8.1.

Item 5 is the only metric that is identified with the paper’s estimand. Items 1–4 are supporting diagnostics. Perspectives (CORNETO, Boolean, ChIP/ATAC, GRETA mechanistic) stay out of the scorecard until the corresponding data or simulator is in data/processed/.
