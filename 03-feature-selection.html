3  Feature selection for cell-type Gaussians

This chapter selects a gene panel \(\mathcal{G}\) on which DeCovarT can discriminate gastruloid cell types through both mean shifts \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j}\) and precision rewiring \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}\equiv\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}^{-1}\). Notation follows the article: gene \(g=1,\ldots,G\); cell type \(j=1,\ldots,J\); bulk \(\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i}\) is a Gaussian convolution of purified profiles (Wang et al. 2019). Feature selection is a pre-modelling filter (Guyon and Elisseeff 2003), not a marker-enrichment score.

The first pipeline treated sctransform highly variable genes (HVGs), optional DESeq2 on technical pseudo-bulks, and an NSGA-II Pareto refinement as a single stack. That mix is too optimistic on Suppinger, where there is only one scRNA-seq resource (GEO GSE229513; (Suppinger et al. 2023)), and it puts variance stabilisation on the same design axis as the graphical model (see Chapter 6 and Note 6.1). Here we split the problem into a naive screen (cheap, marginally motivated) and an all-versus-all refinement (geometry of \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}}\) and rewiring of \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}\)). INDEED and D-trace enter only as a perspective (Chapter 7).

4 Notation

Let \(\boldsymbol{\mu}=(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j})_{j=1}^{J}\in\mathbb{R}^{G\times J}\) be the mean signature and \(\{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}\) the cell-type covariances. Conditionally on proportions \(\boldsymbol{p}\in\Delta^{J-1}\),

\[ \boldsymbol{y}\mid(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\boldsymbol{p}) \sim \mathcal{N}_{G}\!\left( \boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p},\, \sum_{j=1}^{J}p_{j}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j} \right), \quad \boldsymbol{\zeta}=(\boldsymbol{\mu},\{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\}), \tag{4.1}\]

which is the DeCovarT convolution (article Methods; the same \(p_{j}^{2}\) scaling appears in the first pipeline’s aggregation algebra, corrected in Chapter 8). For a subset \(\mathcal{G}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,G\}\) write \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}}\) for the corresponding rows. The 2-norm condition number is

\[ \kappa_{2}(\boldsymbol{X}) = \frac{\sigma_{\max}(\boldsymbol{X})}{\sigma_{\min}(\boldsymbol{X})}. \tag{4.2}\]

Large \(\kappa_{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}})\) is multicollinearity of the mean design (Gong and Szustakowski 2013; Newman et al. 2015). It does not see precision rewiring. For a gastruloid continuum, neighbouring states are close in mean by construction; aggressive \(\kappa\)-minimisation then keeps a few lineage representatives and drops genes whose signal lives in \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}\) (Wang et al. 2019).

Important 4.1: :warning: Keep the mixture on a linear scale

Equation 4.1 is linear in \(\boldsymbol{p}\) only if \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\) and \(\boldsymbol{y}\) live on the same additive scale (counts, CPM, or TPM). Logarithms, SCTransform Pearson residuals, and nonparanormal scores break that map (Sturm et al. 2019; Jin and Liu 2021; Hoffmann et al. 2006). Use residualisation only to rank HVGs (Section 5.1); export DeCovarT moments on the linear scale. The probabilistic reason is Jensen’s inequality: see Note 6.1.

5 Naive screen

The naive stage builds a working universe \(\mathcal{G}_{0}\) of a few thousand genes. It is one-versus-rest in spirit: each gene is scored marginally. That is cheap and biologically readable, and it is the right place to stop if the all-versus-all optimiser has nothing to search over. It is the wrong place to stop for DeCovarT, because concatenating per-type shortlists typically yields a multicollinear \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}}\) (Newman et al. 2015).

5.1 Highly variable genes

Single-cell UMI tables mix depth noise with biological heterogeneity. Regularised negative-binomial regression in sctransform (Hafemeister et al. 2025) / Seurat::SCTransform separates the two via Pearson residuals (Hafemeister and Satija 2019)

\[ r_{cg} = \frac{y_{cg}-\hat{\mu}_{cg}} {\sqrt{\hat{\mu}_{cg}+\hat{\mu}_{cg}^{2}/\hat{\theta}_{g}}}, \tag{5.1}\]

with library size as an offset and empirical-Bayes shrinkage of dispersions \(\hat{\theta}_{g}\). Residual variance is a ranking score for HVGs, not a Gaussian observation model for graphical lasso.

Practical rules for Suppinger:

  1. Do not regress out cell type when ranking HVGs: that removes the inter-type signal the panel is meant to keep.
  2. A design \(\sim\texttt{CellType}\) without intercept contrasts each labelled type against the global mean across types, which is closer to an all-versus-all HVG ranking than a global intercept.
  3. Keep on the order of \(2{,}000\) residual-variable genes as \(\mathcal{G}_{0}\) before any GGM; estimating \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}\) on the full transcriptome is unstable when \(G\gg n_{j}\) even with glasso (Friedman et al. 2008).
  4. SCTransform is tied to HVG ranking, not a factorial “normalisation arm” of the GRN design (Chapter 6).
Note 5.1: :information_source: HVGs are not a GGM input

Pearson residuals are closer to homoscedastic than raw UMI counts, which is why they are popular before PCA. They are still a nonlinear residualisation. Feeding them indiscriminately into SILGGM silently changes the estimand of \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}\) away from the latent Gaussian that DeCovarT wants at the population level. If a Gaussian GGM is used, prefer a copula / nonparanormal map on the same gene set (Section 7.1), or better a Poisson-lognormal latent Gaussian (Section 7.2).

5.2 Marker genes

Classical signatures score gene \(g\) for type \(j\) against the remaining types, then concatenate lists (Newman et al. 2015). Two specificity scores on the simplex of relative means

\[ \pi_{gj} = \frac{\mu_{gj}}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{J}\mu_{g\ell}}, \qquad \sum_{j}\pi_{gj}=1, \tag{5.2}\]

are useful compaction tools, not all-versus-all selectors.

Shannon entropy \(H(g)=-\sum_{j}\pi_{gj}\log\pi_{gj}\) is small when mass concentrates on few types. Gini of the same shares is large when one type dominates. High Gini / low entropy flags one-versus-rest markers. They do not guarantee that the corresponding rows of \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\) are linearly independent: two markers that separate different types can still be collinear as rows if those types co-express a shared programme. Concatenating the top-\(k\) genes per type therefore often inflates \(|\mathcal{G}|\) without improving \(\kappa_{2}\) (see Note 5.3).

Known gastruloid lineage markers (from the Suppinger atlas and companion literature) belong in \(\mathcal{G}_{0}\) as seeds, not as the whole panel. They are the natural starting points for Markov-boundary expansion once a GGM exists (Chapter 7).

5.3 Differential expression — only with biological replication

DESeq2, edgeR and limma–voom remain the workhorses of one-versus-rest ranking (Love et al. 2014). CIBERSORT-style pipelines keep genes with a loose adjusted \(p\) ordered by fold-change, then prune by \(\kappa\) (Newman et al. 2015). That logic does not transfer to Suppinger as a DE experiment.

Warning 5.2: :warning: Technical pseudo-bulks are not biological replicates

Cells from a common experimental unit are not independent biological replicates. Splitting or bootstrapping cells characterises technical / sampling variability; it does not manufacture between-organoid uncertainty. Treating cells, or random partitions of cells, as DESeq2 replicates inflates apparent degrees of freedom and produces false discoveries (Squair et al. 2021; Crowell et al. 2020). The improved pipeline is explicit: Suppinger is one technical scRNA-seq resource.

Use DESeq2 in this book only if independent biological replicates exist (distinct organoids / donors / experiments with the same labelled types). For GSE229513, skip calibrated DE \(p\)-values. Rank genes by residual variance, Gini / entropy, or a nested-model \(F\) statistic treated as a score, not as a \(p\)-value.

If a later gastruloid cohort with genuine replication becomes available (GSE250136 is a different stem-embryo resource, useful as external validation, not as extra Suppinger replicates), then donor-level pseudo-bulks are the correct DE unit (Crowell et al. 2020; Squair et al. 2021). Until then, the naive screen is HVG + markers, not HVG + DESeq2.

Warning 5.3: :warning: Limitation of one-versus-rest concatenation

The union of per-type markers is usually a multicollinear \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}}\) that is still too large for stable NNLS / GLS / DeCovarT Fisher information (Newman et al. 2015). A global all-versus-all stage is required (Chapter 6).

6 All-versus-all refinement

The advanced stage treats \(\mathcal{G}\) as a subset selection problem on the full \(J\)-column signature. The loss is defined on all types jointly. That is the right geometry for DeCovarT: the likelihood depends on \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p}\) and on \(\sum_{j}p_{j}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\), so genes that only separate one type from “the rest” can still leave two gastruloid neighbours unidentifiable.

6.1 Why mean-only \(\kappa\) is not enough

\(\kappa_{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}})\) diagnoses mean collinearity. In a gastruloid continuum it is intrinsically large. Two closely related types can share nearly collinear means and still differ in \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}\). Mean-only pruning then under-represents adjacent or low-abundance states — exactly the types whose rewiring DeCovarT is meant to exploit. Use \(\kappa\) as a diagnostic, not as the sole selector.

A variance-weighted variant standardises rows by gene-wise uncertainties \(\tau_{g}^{2}\) and evaluates conditioning in the \((J-1)\)-dimensional contrast space induced by \(\sum_{j}p_{j}=1\) (package vignette supp-S6-feature-selection.qmd). Unweighted \(\kappa\) can favour quiet but biased genes (Gong and Szustakowski 2013).

6.2 Genetic algorithms

AutoGeneS searches subsets with a multi-objective genetic algorithm that minimises inter-population correlation while maximising centroid distance (Aliee and Theis 2021). The loss is already all-versus-all. Related multi-objective genetic searches appear in network module discovery; they are not DeCovarT-specific.

For this repository the planned optimiser is NSGA-II via mco::nsga2() (Mersmann et al. 2020), as in the package vignette. Encode genes as a mask in \([0,1]^{|\mathcal{G}_{0}|}\) (thresholded to a target size) or as a lower-dimensional embedding of gene blocks. Minimise at least

  1. \(\kappa_{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}})\) (mean geometry);
  2. average pairwise overlap \(\overline{\omega}\) of the \(J\) Gaussians \(\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j})\) (Melnykov et al. 2012, 2021).

MixSim defines pairwise overlap as the sum of the two misclassification probabilities under a Bayes / MAP rule, not as \(1\) minus a distance. That is a sharper estimand than “one minus Hellinger”. Optional analytic surrogates — Jeffreys (symmetric KL), Chernoff / Bhattacharyya, and the Bures–Wasserstein metric on \((\boldsymbol{\mu}_{j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j})\) — belong on the same Pareto front (package vignette). They use both means and covariances, which AutoGeneS correlation / centroid objectives do not.

Note 6.1: :information_source: A DeCovarT-specific third objective (proposal)

The improved pipeline proposed adding Fisher identifiability of \(\boldsymbol{p}\) under Equation 4.1: maximise the smallest eigenvalue of the mixture information \(I_{\mathcal{G}}(\boldsymbol{p})\), or minimise its condition number. That objective is not in the literature corpus; it is a composition. It is the right scientific target — DeCovarT’s score depends on \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p}\) and \(\sum_{j}p_{j}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\) jointly — but it is proportion-dependent and more expensive than MixSim overlap. Keep it as a sensitivity objective on a shortlist of non-dominated panels, not as the only fitness. Do not collapse the front to a single “knee” without nested, leakage-free validation.

Keep several non-dominated panels. Report the whole front. Nested cross-validation, when biological replicates exist, must build HVGs, DEGs and the Pareto panel inside each outer fold; otherwise the panel leaks (Squair et al. 2021). With one Suppinger resource, the honest statement is: the panel is fitted on this atlas and must be challenged on an external gastruloid cohort.

6.3 Differential networks as candidate generators

DeCovarT benefits specifically from rewiring between populations. A gene that changes neighbourhood in \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}\) is a better candidate than a gene that is merely highly variable. Two strategies exist.

Fit every \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}\), then compare supports. Independent glasso / SILGGM fits (Section 7.1) yield per-type edges; the symmetric difference of supports, or the difference of partial correlations, ranks genes by differential connectivity. This is simple and matches the objects DeCovarT eventually needs, but each glasso is shrunk and the difference of two biased estimators is not a debiased estimator of \(\Delta_{j\ell}=\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\ell}\).

Estimate \(\Delta_{j\ell}\) directly. D-trace methods target the difference of precisions without necessarily reconstructing both graphs first (see Chapter 7). They cannot replace \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}\) in the generative model — DeCovarT still needs each covariance individually — but they are attractive feature objectives when individual networks are fairly dense and the difference is sparse.

Either way, treat differential-network hits as candidates for the all-versus-all stage, not as the final panel. Concatenating one-versus-rest network genes repeats Note 5.3.

7 Perspectives: INDEED and D-trace

These methods are not in the core Suppinger pipeline. They are the statistically natural next step when (i) two (or \(J\)) groups are to be compared as networks, and (ii) genes that both shift in mean and rewire are the desired biomarkers. Full detail lives in DeCovarT vignettes/supp-S6-feature-selection.qmd; we only import the estimands.

7.1 INDEED: an ad-hoc fusion of DE and differential partial correlation

INDEED fits a sparse precision per group, converts to partial correlations

\[ \mathrm{pc}_{gg'}^{(j)} = -\frac{\theta_{gg'}^{(j)}} {\sqrt{\theta_{gg}^{(j)}\,\theta_{g'g'}^{(j)}}}, \qquad \Delta\mathrm{pc}_{gg'} = \mathrm{pc}_{gg'}^{(j)}-\mathrm{pc}_{gg'}^{(\ell)}, \tag{7.1}\]

filters \(\Delta\mathrm{pc}\) with a permutation (or bootstrap) null, and forms an activity score that multiplies mean evidence by differential connectivity (Zuo et al. 2016)

\[ \mathrm{AS}_{g} = \bigl(-\log p_{g}^{\mathrm{DE}}\bigr) \sum_{g'\neq g} \bigl\lvert\Delta\mathrm{pc}_{gg'}\bigr\rvert \, \mathbf{1}\{\Delta\mathrm{pc}_{gg'}\ \text{significant}\}. \tag{7.2}\]

Relative to pure DGE, this favours genes whose undirected neighbourhood (Markov boundary in the GGM) differs between groups. Related ideas appear in weighted glasso pipelines with PPI priors.

Criticism for Suppinger. The DE \(p\)-value in Equation 7.2 assumes biological replication (Note 5.2). The permutation null on \(\Delta\mathrm{pc}\) among cells of one gastruloid is again a technical null. INDEED is also ad hoc: it tests edges after two separate glasso fits rather than estimating \(\Delta\) under a joint loss. Use it, if at all, as a ranking heuristic on \(\mathcal{G}_{0}\), with \(p_{g}^{\mathrm{DE}}\) replaced by a fold-change or \(F\) score.

7.2 D-trace: a loss that targets \(\Delta=\Theta_{j}-\Theta_{\ell}\)

Zhang and Zou (Zhang and Zou 2014) introduced the lasso-penalised D-trace loss for a single sparse precision, with an alternating-direction algorithm and sparse-recovery guarantees under an irrepresentability condition that can hold when the analogous condition for lasso-penalised Gaussian likelihood fails. In the same paper they note that the graphical lasso estimator of a sparse precision is usually still consistent for the support of the true direct interactions even when the data are not multivariate Gaussian. That is useful background for our glasso / SILGGM branch: off-Gaussian UMI tables need not destroy edge recovery, even though they do destroy the interpretation of \(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{j}\) as a Gaussian generative precision.

The differential-network extension (Yuan et al. 2017) estimates a sparse difference \(\Delta_{j\ell}=\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\ell}\). Empirically, D-trace is competitive for differential-network recovery when the difference is sparser than either graph, although hubs and dense networks remain difficult (Plaksienko et al. 2025).

For DeCovarT this is the cleaner perspective than INDEED:

  • the estimand \(\Delta_{j\ell}\) is exactly “which partial correlations rewire”;
  • it does not require a calibrated DE \(p\)-value;
  • it still does not produce a pair of SPD \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j},\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\ell}\) suitable as generative covariances.

A workable composition — labelled as a proposal, not as a result — is: use D-trace (or debiased fused graphical lasso) to nominate rewiring genes; retain those genes in \(\mathcal{G}\); then estimate each \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}\) on \(\mathcal{G}\) with a method from Chapter 8 that returns an SPD precision.

7.3 Markov boundaries as panel expansion

Once a GGM exists, a small set of seeds (markers, HVG hubs, INDEED / D-trace hits) can be grown by undirected neighbours \(\{g':\theta_{gg'}\neq 0\}\). In a Gaussian Markov random field those neighbours are the Markov boundary: they carry residual information about \(g\) given the rest of the genes. This is undirected conditioning, not DAG \(d\)-separation. Directed path-blocking rules are the wrong language for glasso supports.

8 What we would actually run on Suppinger

  1. Naive screen. SCTransform (or residual variance from a depth-aware NB) to rank HVGs; add curated gastruloid markers; no DESeq2 \(p\)-values on technical pseudo-bulks.
  2. Cap \(|\mathcal{G}_{0}|\). A few thousand genes before any GGM.
  3. All-versus-all. NSGA-II on \(\kappa_{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}})\) and MixSim \(\overline{\omega}\), optionally with Jeffreys / Chernoff as diagnostics. Keep the Pareto set.
  4. Optional DiffNet candidates. Symmetric difference of per-type SILGGM supports, or D-trace on a pair of abundant types, as extra genes in \(\mathcal{G}_{0}\) — not as the panel.
  5. Export linear-scale \(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}}\) and \(\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}\) into DeCovarT.

The first pipeline’s “HVG then DESeq2 then NSGA-II on \(\kappa\) and overlap” is directionally right but overstates what one gastruloid scRNA-seq experiment can support, and it treats variance stabilisation as if it were a harmless preprocessing for the mixture. The improved pipeline’s Fisher-information objective is scientifically tighter and should be treated as a proposal until it is computed.
