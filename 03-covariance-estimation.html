4.1 Scope and status

Numbers to rerun: they were computed on the panel of the earlier tier (i), which dropped the 2,178 mostly fractional genes and used the 5 % presence gate. Tier (i) now keeps every gene and applies the gate of Equation 3.1 (Section 3.2.1).

This chapter estimates, for each scored cell type j at each bulk-matched time, the mean \widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j} and the within-type covariance \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} of the gene panel of tier (ii) (Section 3.3), and exports them on the linear scale of DeCovarT’s convolution,

\bigl\{ \widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j},\, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j},\, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j}=\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}^{-1} \bigr\}_{j=1}^{J}, \qquad \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}\succ 0 . \tag{4.1}

The target is the covariance that enters the likelihood, not a mechanistic gene network; a dense estimate is therefore an acceptable plug-in (3.2, why a regularised covariance).

What is run. scripts/03_05_stats_plugin_covariance.R stores the sample moments of every type; it regularises nothing. The deconvolution builds each covariance from these moments with build_sigma(), for one arm and one tuning value. Every arm keeps the sample variances and regularises the correlation (Section 4.2). The tuning values are chosen here, on held-out cells (Section 4.3), or by the bulk likelihood (Section 5.3.2), always by grid search. The sparse-precision arms and the denoised covariance are archived (Section 4.2.4). The theory is in 3.2, estimators.

4.1.1 Inputs and scales

Every arm reads the raw UMI counts of the scored cells, element slots[[time]] of output/feature_selection/tier_01/scored_slots.rds (Section 3.2.1): counts (genes × cells), cell_type, batch and library_size, the full RNA library of each cell. The counts are restricted to the genes of output/feature_selection/tier_02/selected_<time>.csv, the panel gated by the permuted Welch G at BH 0.05 (Section 3.3), the Welch panel: 1,360 genes at 48 h, 3,617 at 72 h and 5,893 at 96 h, with no cap. read_panel_slot() does the subset and returns cells × genes. The 500-gene legacy export of Section 3.6 is no longer read.

Three gene sets per time are listed in output/grn/plugin/gene_sets.csv: welch, the whole gated panel; r2_01, its genes with a deviance share R^2_D\ge 1\% (150, 978 and 2,991 genes at 48, 72 and 96 h); and r2_05, those with R^2_D\ge 5\% (17, 148 and 685 genes). Results in this chapter are on the Welch panel unless stated. For each time and gene set, sample_moments() writes the mean of each type (mu), its unbiased sample covariance, not regularised (sigma_sample), its number of cells (n_cells), and the pooled within-type correlation of the time (r_pool, Section 4.2.2). plugin_summary.csv lists, per unit, the cells, the genes and the genes constant within the type; on the Welch panel no gene is constant within a type.

Normalisation. The exported means and covariances are computed on raw per-cell UMI counts, with no per-cell library division (normalise = "none"). Dividing each cell by its total library removes the type-level transcriptome size S_j: signatures become mRNA fractions, and the proportions are estimated as q_j\propto p_jS_j instead of p_j (Lu et al. 2025, 13). It also removes the biological cell size variation between cells of one type, which the bulk accumulates. Without spike-ins the technical size factor cannot be separated from the biological one, so the size term of the covariance was bracketed by the coefficient \kappa of the denoised estimator, now archived (Section 4.2.4): \kappa=0 treats all size variation as biological; \kappa=\hat\nu/(1+\hat\nu) treats it all as technical, which matches library normalisation for the covariance. The argument is in 3.2, raw or denoised. The earlier per-cell division, X <- y / (lib / ref_lib) with ref_lib the median library of the time point, was a sensitivity variant (normalise = "library") of the archived code. The archived PLNnetwork arm kept its full-library offset \log s_c in both cases.

Size term removed alone. sample_moments(..., library_size = ) gives the “all technical” end of the \kappa bracket without the rest of the denoised estimator. The mean is kept on raw counts; the covariance is computed on the cells of type j divided by their size factor L_c/\bar L_j, with L_c the full library of cell c and \bar L_j its mean over the type. That removes the size term \nu\,\mu_g\mu_h and keeps the Poisson diagonal, which the archived denoised covariance also removed. This is the size-normalised input of the scale screen of chapter 4 (Section 5.3.5).

Means and covariances are computed on the linear counts, never on \log(1+x): the convolution is linear only on an additive scale (2.1, normalisation). An earlier glasso script fitted and exported on \log(1+x); that choice was reverted before it was archived.

4.1.2 Units and regimes

Each fit uses the cells of one type at one time, treated as independent draws: eleven type × time units (Table 3.2). Cell line is not a covariate (Section 2.1). The panel has G_t genes at time t, before genes constant within a type are removed. Table 4.2 sets the C_j cells of each unit against three sample-size layers (3.2, regimes): existence of the maximum-likelihood estimate of \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}, support recovery by the graphical lasso, and support recovery by neighbourhood selection.

In 9 of the 11 units C_j\le G_t: the sample covariance is singular, and only a regularised estimate exists. Only the epiblast at 48 h (10,590 cells against 1,360 genes) and primitive streak at 72 h (4,279 cells against 3,617 genes) have a maximum-likelihood estimate. The graphical-lasso order is exceeded by 4 units and the neighbourhood-selection order by 8; the 48 h naïve pluripotency, the 72 h naïve pluripotency and the 96 h somite differentiation front exceed neither. The cross-validated score of Section 4.3 is defined in every regime because each arm returns a positive definite matrix.

4.1.3 Bulk depth per cell

Which covariance enters the bulk law, raw or denoised, depends on the bulk depth per cell relative to the reference (3.2, raw or denoised): the raw covariance is exact only at a_i=1. For bulk sample i,

a_i=\frac{y_{+i}/n_i}{\bar s_{\mathrm{ref}}}, \tag{4.2}

with y_{+i} the total counts of the bulk sample, n_i the number of cells in it, and \bar s_{\mathrm{ref}} the median UMIs per reference cell at the matched time; a_i is the capture ratio of the sample. a_i<1 is expected: the bulk library is deeper in total than one droplet, but it spreads that depth over every cell of the pooled sample. Bulk counts are reads, not UMIs, so their sampling noise is overdispersed, with a dispersion \omega_i\ge1 that lowers the effective depth to a_i/\omega_i. The raw plug-in, the implemented default, assumes a_i=1. n_i is TODO: cells per gastruloid × gastruloids per bulk sample, from the Suppinger et al. 2023 methods (Suppinger et al. 2023, STAR Methods, p. 25). How a count n_i would enter the scale models of the deconvolution is in Section 5.3.4.

4.2 Estimators as run

Every arm keeps the sample variances and regularises the correlation only. With \boldsymbol{D}_j the sample variances and \boldsymbol{R}_j the sample correlation of type j at one time,

\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} = \boldsymbol{D}_j^{1/2}\,\widetilde{\boldsymbol{R}}_j\,\boldsymbol{D}_j^{1/2}, \tag{4.3}

with \widetilde{\boldsymbol{R}}_j one of the forms of Table 4.3. No arm penalises the diagonal; the reason is in 3.2, diagonal. build_sigma() builds Equation 4.3 from the stored moments. In the shrinkage arms a gene with no variance in a type gets 10^{-4} times the median variance of that type. The factor arm uses the scale of its own fit, the Poisson floor of Section 4.3, so that \boldsymbol{B}_j\boldsymbol{B}_j^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_j maps back to the variances the factors were fitted on. The shrinkage plug-in of the deconvolution is unchanged by that floor.

4.2.1 Shrinkage covariance

(1-\lambda)\boldsymbol{R}_j+\lambda\boldsymbol{I} is the Schäfer–Strimmer target D: variances kept, every correlation multiplied by 1-\lambda (Schäfer and Strimmer 2005) TODO-cite: Schäfer and Strimmer 2005, target D; p. to check. \lambda, the shrinkage intensity, is chosen on held-out cells (Section 4.3), either per type or as one level common to the types of a time. The closed-form level is archived (Section 4.2.4). The estimate is positive definite for every \lambda>0, whatever C_j and G_t; the smallest level of the grid is 10^{-3}. The theory is in 3.2, shrinkage.

4.2.2 Two-parameter shrinkage

\bar{\boldsymbol{R}} is the correlation of \boldsymbol{W}=\sum_j (C_j-1)\boldsymbol{V}_j/(N-J), with \boldsymbol{V}_j the sample covariance of type j and N the cells of the J types of one time and one gene set (pooled_correlation()). \boldsymbol{W} is the tied covariance of linear discriminant analysis. \alpha moves \boldsymbol{R}_j towards \bar{\boldsymbol{R}}; \lambda then moves the result towards \boldsymbol{I}. This is the family of regularised discriminant analysis TODO-cite: Friedman 1989, Regularized discriminant analysis, Journal of the American Statistical Association; p. to check TODO-cite: Hastie, Tibshirani and Friedman 2009, The Elements of Statistical Learning, 2nd ed., Springer; ch. 4, sec. 4.3.1, p. 112, eqs. 4.13–4.14. In cross-validation \bar{\boldsymbol{R}} never contains the held-out cells of the type being scored.

\bar{\boldsymbol{R}} alone is not an arm. It has G_t(G_t-1)/2 free correlations, 0.9 to 17.4 million, for 4,611 to 10,891 cells per time. At 96 h it is singular: \boldsymbol{W} has rank at most N-J = 4,930, for 5,893 genes. It enters only as a target, with \alpha<1 and \lambda>0.

4.2.3 Factor analysis

fit_factor_correlation() fits \boldsymbol{R}_j\approx\boldsymbol{B}_j\boldsymbol{B}_j^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_j with q_j factors by maximum likelihood, with the EM steps of Rubin and Thayer TODO-cite: Rubin and Thayer 1982, EM algorithms for ML factor analysis, Psychometrika; p. to check. The columns of \boldsymbol{B}_j are the factor loadings and the diagonal \boldsymbol{\Psi}_j holds the uniquenesses. The steps are written on the cells, so the G\times G correlation is never formed. The cells are standardised with the Poisson floor of Section 4.3. The start is the probabilistic PCA solution; EM stops after 200 steps. Uniquenesses are floored at 0.01: with fewer cells than genes the likelihood is unbounded when one of them tends to zero. Rank 0 is the diagonal model.

fit_factor_shared() fits \boldsymbol{R}_j\approx\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_j: the types of one time share the loadings and keep their own uniquenesses (“same axes, own volumes”). It alternates closed-form updates of the rows of \boldsymbol{B} and of each \boldsymbol{\Psi}_j, for at most 100 steps.

A factor model on G genes has G+qG-q(q-1)/2 free covariance parameters: loadings up to rotation, plus uniquenesses TODO-cite: Murphy 2012, Machine Learning: A Probabilistic Perspective, MIT Press; ch. 12, sec. 12.1.3, p. 384. Shared loadings are counted once, plus one uniqueness vector per type. A rank is used only if it is at most the training cells minus two, at most the rank of the training correlation, and leaves no more parameters than the G(G+1)/2 entries of the matrix. Rank 30 is therefore never fitted for the 29-cell somite differentiation front.

4.2.4 Archived arms

These arms are not run in the current pipeline. Their code and a README are in scripts/archives/sparse_precision_arms/ and scripts/archives/denoised_covariance/.

Sparse precisions. The graphical lasso with an unpenalised diagonal (glasso, EBIC path), the SILGGM support with a refit, and PLNnetwork, a Poisson log-normal model with the full-library offset \log s_c and a latent precision \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_z^{-1} penalised off the diagonal:

x_{gc}\mid\boldsymbol{z}_{\cdot c}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}\bigl(s_{c}e^{z_{gc}}\bigr), \qquad \boldsymbol{z}_{\cdot c}\sim\mathcal{N}_{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{z},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}). \tag{4.4}

None was run on the gated panel. The pipeline now regularises covariances only; these precision estimators may return as a sensitivity analysis. Theory: 3.2, graphical lasso, 3.2, PLN.

  • Denoised covariance. The Poisson diagonal and a share \kappa of the size term removed from the sample covariance, \kappa bracketed between 0 and \hat\nu/(1+\hat\nu), with \hat\nu from extrinsic_noise_nu() (3.2, raw or denoised). It gave the worst deconvolution of the second pass: RMSE 0.47 at 96 h, Welch panel. These second-pass results are kept in output/deconvolution/second_pass/; why the input was dropped is in Section 5.7.
  • Closed-form shrinkage level. The Schäfer–Strimmer \hat\lambda^{\ast} of corpcor minimises a Frobenius risk, not the Gaussian likelihood that DeCovarT evaluates. In the first pass it was 0.006 to 0.026 for the 3 units with 3,589 cells or more, and 0.60 to 0.74 for the units with 29 to 66 cells (output/deconvolution/second_pass/plugin_summary_first_pass.csv).

4.3 Cross-validation and outputs

One loss selects every tuning value and compares the arms: the held-out Gaussian log-score on the linear scale. For held-out cells \mathcal{T} and an estimate (\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}) fitted without them,

\ell(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}};\mathcal{T}) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{T}} \log\phi_{G}\bigl(\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c};\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\bigr). \tag{4.5}

Its expectation is maximised at the true mean and covariance whatever the law of the cells (Stein’s loss), so it is a legitimate loss even though cells are not Gaussian (3.2, Gaussian log-score). Why it replaces AIC, BIC and EBIC as the selector is in 3.2, cross-validation. The bulk samples are never read.

Folds. Leave-one-out under 100 cells (the 36 and 66 naïve cells at 48 h and 72 h, the 29-cell somite front), 5-fold otherwise. The two-parameter arm uses 5 folds in every unit. Shared loadings use 5 joint folds, each type split into 5. Folds split the cells of one unit: the score measures generalisation to new cells of this specimen, not to new specimens (Note 3.1).

TODO (reply to Elias, 2026-11-22): complement the held-out log-score by the universal approximate cross-validation (UACV) of Commenges et al. (2015), which approximates leave-one-out without refitting and corrects for a misspecified or penalised fit, and report its tracking interval \Delta_{\mathrm{UACV}}\pm1.96\,\hat\omega/\sqrt{n} when two arms or two models are compared. The interval is asymptotic only, and its unit must be an independent one: cells of one specimen are pseudo-replicates, and three bulk replicates per time are too few for a stable variance.

Poisson floor. split_standardise() (R/03_01_stats_covariance_estimation.R) standardises the training and test cells with the training mean \bar x_g and standard deviation s_g of each gene, with the variance floored at its Poisson level plus one count over the C_{\mathrm{tr}} training cells:

s_g^{2}=\max\Bigl(v_g,\ \bar x_g+\tfrac{1}{C_{\mathrm{tr}}}\Bigr), \tag{4.6}

with v_g the sample variance. A count cannot vary less than a Poisson count; a smaller variance means too few cells, or the fractional counts left by ambient-RNA correction. The floor replaced a defect found in the shared-loadings arm. Before it, the held-out score of the 29-cell somite differentiation front at 96 h (outer fold 2 of the nested comparison) was about -5\times10^{10}. The gene Fnbp1 has fractional ambient-corrected counts in that type: training mean 4\times10^{-7}, standard deviation 1.9\times10^{-6}. One count in a test cell then cost about 6\times10^{11}. With the floor that fold scores −9,260, and the other types move by less than 0.3%. These values come from the diagnosis of the defect, not from the tables below. The factor fits use the same scale (build_sigma(), Section 4.2); the shrinkage plug-in of the deconvolution is unchanged. The tables of Section 4.4 come from the rerun with the floor (25 November 2026, 13:57).

One spectral decomposition per training set. The test cells are standardised with the training means and the floored standard deviations s_g of Equation 4.6. Let \boldsymbol{R}=\sum_{i\le r}\ell_i\boldsymbol{v}_i\boldsymbol{v}_i^{\top} be the training correlation, of rank r on G genes. The eigenvectors of (1-\lambda)\boldsymbol{R}+\lambda\boldsymbol{I} do not depend on \lambda; its eigenvalues are e_i=(1-\lambda)\ell_i+\lambda on the range and \lambda on the null space. For a standardised test cell \boldsymbol{z},

\log\phi = -\tfrac12\Bigl[ G\log 2\pi+2\sum_g\log s_g +\sum_{i\le r}\log e_i+(G-r)\log\lambda +\sum_{i\le r}\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}_i^{\top}\boldsymbol{z})^{2}}{e_i} +\frac{\lVert\boldsymbol{z}\rVert^{2}-\sum_{i\le r}(\boldsymbol{v}_i^{\top}\boldsymbol{z})^{2}}{\lambda} \Bigr]. \tag{4.7}

One decomposition therefore scores the 43 levels (spectral_path_core()). It is taken on the smaller of \boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{Z}^{\top} and \boldsymbol{Z}^{\top}\boldsymbol{Z}, with \boldsymbol{Z} the standardised training cells. The two-parameter arm needs one decomposition per \alpha.

Factor scores. With \boldsymbol{M}=\boldsymbol{I}+\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{-1}\boldsymbol{B}, of size q\times q,

(\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi})^{-1} = \boldsymbol{\Psi}^{-1}-\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{-1}\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{-1}, \qquad \det(\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}) = \det\boldsymbol{\Psi}\,\det\boldsymbol{M} \tag{4.8}

(factor_core()). No G\times G matrix is factorised.

Constant genes. A gene constant in a training set is scored apart, as an independent Gaussian with the variance of Equation 4.6. That part of the score is the same for every tuning value; it is left out of the fold-to-fold spread.

Rules.

  • Per type: the value with the highest mean held-out score (min). The one-standard-error choice, the most regularised value within one standard error of that maximum, is reported beside it (1se).
  • Common: one value per time, the one with the highest sum over types of the mean held-out score. For shared loadings this is the only rule.

Nested comparison. A nested cross-validation: the cells of each time are split into 5 outer folds, stratified by type. Each rule chooses its tuning value on the outer training cells only, with the folds above, and is scored on the outer test cells. Scores are paired by fold and type; the gain is reported against the diagonal model.

Information criteria. The factor models are refitted on all cells of each unit (stage fit; these fits enter the deconvolution). AIC and BIC are computed from the free-parameter counts of Table 4.3, plus G means per type. They are reported, not used to select.

Grid search only. Every tuning value is chosen on a fixed grid. With one or two tuning parameters a grid is as reliable as random search, whose advantage appears when few of many hyperparameters matter TODO-cite: Bergstra and Bengio 2012, Random search for hyper-parameter optimization, Journal of Machine Learning Research 13:281–305; p. 283. Here the shrinkage “path” is itself a grid, of 43 levels, scored from one spectral decomposition per fold (Equation 4.7); the two-parameter arm adds one decomposition per \alpha. The former search stage compared, on 5 folds, the exact path on 200 levels, a 10-value grid with one Cholesky factor per value, optimize() on the path over [10^{-3},1] and 10 random levels; for the two-parameter arm, on panels of at most 1,500 genes, a 5\times8 grid in (\alpha,\lambda) against 40 random pairs. It is archived in scripts/archives/search_comparison/ (see its README); its table, output/grn/cv/search_comparison.csv, is still read here (Section 4.4.3). The loss of a strategy is the held-out score of the best path value minus that of its choice.

\begin{algorithm} \caption{Held-out scores along the paths, selection, and nested comparison} \begin{algorithmic} \Procedure{PathScores}{$X_{\mathrm{train}}, X_{\mathrm{test}}, \Lambda, Q, A$} \State standardise both sets with the training means and standard deviations \State $(\ell, V) \gets$ non-zero spectrum of the training correlation $R$ \For{$\lambda \in \Lambda$} \State $s(\lambda) \gets$ mean path score of the test cells, from $(\ell, V)$ \EndFor \For{$q \in Q$} \State $(B, \Psi) \gets$ \Call{FactorEM}{$X_{\mathrm{train}}, q$}, started from $(\ell, V)$ \State $s(q) \gets$ mean test score through the Woodbury identity \EndFor \For{$\alpha \in A$} \State spectrum of $(1-\alpha)R + \alpha\bar R$; $s(\alpha,\lambda)$ for every $\lambda \in \Lambda$ \EndFor \State \Return $s$ \EndProcedure \Procedure{Select}{$X$, folds} \For{each fold $k$} \State $s_k \gets$ \Call{PathScores}{$X_{-k}, X_k, \Lambda, Q, A$} \EndFor \State $m \gets$ mean of $s_k$ over folds \State \Return $\arg\max m$ per type; $\arg\max \sum_j m_j$ for a common value \EndProcedure \Procedure{NestedCompare}{$X_1,\ldots,X_J$} \State split each $X_j$ into 5 outer folds \For{$k = 1,\ldots,5$} \For{each rule $e$} \State choose its value by \Call{Select}{} on the outer training cells \State $g_{ek} \gets$ test score of rule $e$ $-$ test score of the diagonal \EndFor \EndFor \State \Return $(g_{ek})$, paired by fold \EndProcedure \end{algorithmic} \end{algorithm}

4.4 Results and discussion

4.4.1 Regularisation shrinks covariance terms without setting them to zero

What the shrinkage arm returns. The estimate is \boldsymbol{D}_j^{1/2}\{(1-\lambda)\boldsymbol{R}_j+\lambda\boldsymbol{I}\}\boldsymbol{D}_j^{1/2} (Section 4.2.1). Three readings follow.

  • Variances are untouched. Only the correlations move.
  • Every correlation is multiplied by 1-\lambda; none becomes zero. The estimate has as many non-zero off-diagonal terms as the sample covariance: all G_t(G_t-1)/2 pairs, that is 0.9 million at 48 h, 6.5 million at 72 h and 17.4 million at 96 h on the Welch panel. Its inverse is dense as well, so no graph can be read from either matrix. The factor models are dense too: each off-diagonal term is an entry of \boldsymbol{B}_j\boldsymbol{B}_j^{\top}.
  • \lambda is a floor on the spectrum. Each eigenvalue \ell of \boldsymbol{R}_j becomes (1-\lambda)\ell+\lambda, so the smallest eigenvalue of the shrunk correlation is at least \lambda. That floor makes the estimate positive definite when C_j\le G_t.
  • Held-out cells choose strong shrinkage. The per-type level is 0.44 for the 48 h epiblast, 0.84 to 0.91 for the other types with 1,000 cells or more, and 0.88 to 0.97 for the types with fewer. Except in the 48 h epiblast, each sample correlation keeps 3% to 16% of its value. The common level is 0.88 at 48 h, 0.94 at 72 h and 0.91 at 96 h. The 1se rule picks \lambda=1, the diagonal, for no unit.
  • The two-parameter arm pools as much as the grid allows. \alpha=0.75, the largest value of the grid, in 20 of the 22 Welch choices, per type and common. Its \lambda is no higher than that of plain shrinkage: 0.44 to 0.88 per type.
  • Factor ranks follow the number of cells. Rank 30, the edge of the grid, for the 48 h epiblast, the 72 h primitive streak and the 96 h pre-somitic mesoderm; ranks 1 to 5 for the types with fewer than 1,000 cells. Shared loadings choose rank 20 at 48 h, 20 at 72 h and 20 at 96 h.
  • The shared rank and the Poisson floor. Before the floor of Equation 4.6, the shared-loadings scores of the two rarest types were of order -10^{7} (36 naïve cells, 48 h) and -10^{10} (somite front, 96 h) at every rank, rank 0 included, against -2.4\times10^{3} and -2.2\times10^{4} for the same diagonal model scored by leave-one-out. The somite front then decided the sum over types and set the 96 h shared rank. The cause at 96 h was one gene with fractional counts (Section 4.3); the 48 h case was not traced separately. With the floor (tables of 25 November 2026, 13:57) shared loadings choose rank 20, the largest of their grid, at 48 h, 72 h and 96 h on the Welch and R^2_D\ge 1\% panels (Table 4.5). The rank is not interior there; the grid must be extended before the shared loadings are compared with the other arms.
  • The common level is far above what the bulk chooses. Held-out cells choose a common shrinkage level of 0.66 to 0.94 across panels and times (Table 4.5). The bulk likelihood chooses 0.10 to 0.50 on the Welch and R^2_D\ge 1\% panels, and 1.00 at 48 h, 0.10 at 72 h and 0.25 at 96 h on the R^2_D\ge 5\% panel, whose 48 h slot has 17 genes.
  • Shared loadings sit at the top of their grid, rank 20, in 7 of the 9 slots; the per-type rank is 30, the top of its grid, in 5 of the 33 type × time × panel units.

A dense estimate is not evidence that every pair of genes interacts. Three mechanisms give a non-zero covariance to a pair with no direct link.

  • Sampling noise. A sample correlation is never exactly zero, even for independent genes. Shrinkage multiplies it by 1-\lambda; it does not threshold.
  • Covariance is not the network. Direct interactions are the zeros of the precision matrix. Two genes linked only through other genes still have a non-zero covariance. A covariance block is zero only between groups of genes with no path between them. Isolated groups could be looked for with the archived sparse arms (Section 4.2.4), not with these.
  • Raw counts share a cell-size factor. The covariances are computed on raw counts (Section 4.1.1), so every pair of genes receives the size term \nu\,\mu_g\mu_h, positive for all pairs. This is the extrinsic noise that the archived extrinsic_noise_nu() estimated.

4.4.2 Nested comparison

  • Every rule beats the diagonal on average. The mean gain is positive in 66 of the 66 unit × rule cells, and exceeds two standard errors in 66. The exceptions are none.
  • Abundant types. In the units with 1,000 cells or more the rule with the highest mean gain is: epiblast (48 h), per-type factor analysis (165), primitive streak (72 h), per-type factor analysis (259), pre-somitic mesoderm (96 h), shared loadings (626) and caudal mesoderm (96 h), shared loadings (570).
  • Rare types. In the units with fewer than 1,000 cells: ectopic pluripotency (72 h), shared loadings (211), exiting naïve pluripotency (48 h), shared loadings (137), gut (96 h), shared loadings (522), ectopic pluripotency (96 h), shared loadings (554), naïve pluripotency (72 h), shared loadings (269), naïve pluripotency (48 h), shared loadings (97) and somite differentiation front (96 h), shared loadings (833). In 7 of these 7 units the best rule borrows across types (shared loadings or the two-parameter arm).
  • Common rules and the diagonal. The common shrinkage and two-parameter rules choose \lambda=1, the diagonal, in 0 of their 30 time × rule × outer-fold choices (nested_cv_by_fold.csv); such a fold widens their standard errors.

4.4.3 Search strategies

  • The exact path is the cheapest and the reference. Median 0.13 s per unit, against 2.1 s for a 10-value grid refitted value by value. The refitted grid loses 0.78 log-score units per cell (median); it loses more than 0.1 in 15 of 22 units. Its largest loss, 1,200,000, is for the 48 h naïve pluripotency (welch panel), whose best level is 1 while that grid stops at 0.9. The loss comes from the coarse grid, not from grid search: the path is a finer grid.
  • Random levels lose 0.48 (median) for the same cost as the path.
  • optimize() on the path matches the path in 21 of 22 units (loss at most 0.01; a negative loss is a level between two path values).
  • Two-parameter arm. 40 random pairs take 11.8 s per unit against 1.7 s for the 5\times8 grid, and lose 0.06 (median).
  • On the Welch panel alone the times are larger (median 0.28 s for the path, 44 s for the refitted grid) and so are the losses (median 8.0 for the refitted grid).

4.4.4 Information criteria

On the Welch panel (information_criteria.csv), AIC chooses the largest fitted rank for 8 of 11 types, and rank 20 at 48 h, 20 at 72 h and 20 at 96 h for the shared loadings. BIC chooses ranks 1 to 8 for 10 types, and 20 for the 48 h epiblast. For the shared loadings BIC chooses 20 at 48 h, 8 at 72 h and 12 at 96 h. Of the 98 per-type fits on all cells, 16 stopped at the 200-step limit; 23 of the 24 shared fits converged.

4.4.5 What this implies for the deconvolution

The acceptance of a dense plug-in (Section 4.1) concerns the likelihood, not the interpretation. The levels that predict held-out cells best are too strong for the bulk (Section 5.3.2). On the Welch panel at 96 h the RMSE of the proportions is 0.108 with the per-type levels and 0.155 with the common level, against 0.040 at \lambda=0.5, the level chosen by the bulk likelihood (output/deconvolution/lambda_selection.csv).

### Inputs and scales {#sec-cov-inputs}

Every arm reads the raw UMI counts of the scored cells, element
`slots[[time]]` of `output/feature_selection/tier_01/scored_slots.rds`
(@sec-fs-qc-applied): `counts` (genes × cells), `cell_type`, `batch`
and `library_size`, the full RNA library of each cell. The counts are
restricted to the genes of
`output/feature_selection/tier_02/selected_<time>.csv`, the panel gated
by the permuted Welch $G$ at BH 0.05 (@sec-fs-consistency), the
`r glossary("Welch panel")`: `r big(n_welch[["48h"]])` genes
at 48 h, `r big(n_welch[["72h"]])` at 72 h and
`r big(n_welch[["96h"]])` at 96 h, with no cap.
`read_panel_slot()` does the subset and returns cells × genes. The
500-gene legacy export of @sec-fs-export is no longer read.

Three gene sets per time are listed in `output/grn/plugin/gene_sets.csv`:
`welch`, the whole gated panel; `r2_01`, its genes with a
`r glossary("deviance share")` $R^2_D\ge 1\%$
(`r and_list(big(n_r2_01))` genes at 48, 72 and 96 h); and `r2_05`,
those with $R^2_D\ge 5\%$ (`r and_list(big(n_r2_05))` genes). Results in
this chapter are on the Welch panel unless stated. For each time and gene set,
`sample_moments()` writes the mean of each type (`mu`), its unbiased
sample covariance, not regularised (`sigma_sample`), its number of
cells (`n_cells`), and the pooled within-type correlation of the time
(`r_pool`, @sec-cov-two-param). `plugin_summary.csv` lists, per unit,
the cells, the genes and the genes constant within the type; on the
Welch panel no gene is constant within a type.

**Normalisation.** The exported means and covariances are computed on
raw per-cell UMI counts, with no per-cell library division
(`normalise = "none"`). Dividing each cell by its total library removes
the type-level transcriptome size $S_j$: signatures become mRNA
fractions, and the proportions are estimated as
$q_j\propto p_jS_j$ instead of $p_j$
[@luTranscriptomeSizeMatters2025, p. 13]. It also removes the biological
`r glossary("cell size")` variation between cells of one type, which
the bulk accumulates. Without spike-ins the technical
`r glossary("size factor")` cannot be separated from
the biological one, so the size term of the covariance was bracketed by
the coefficient $\kappa$ of the denoised estimator, now archived
(@sec-cov-archived):
$\kappa=0$ treats all size variation as biological;
$\kappa=\hat\nu/(1+\hat\nu)$ treats it all as technical, which matches
library normalisation for the covariance. The argument is in
[3.2, raw or denoised](https://bastienchassagnol.github.io/DeCovarT/articles/03-2-covariance-network-estimation.html#sec-raw-vs-denoised).
The earlier per-cell division, `X <- y / (lib / ref_lib)` with
`ref_lib` the median library of the time point, was a sensitivity
variant (`normalise = "library"`) of the archived code. The archived
`PLNnetwork` arm kept its full-library offset $\log s_c$ in both cases.

**Size term removed alone.** `sample_moments(..., library_size = )`
gives the "all technical" end of the $\kappa$ bracket without the rest
of the denoised estimator. The mean is kept on raw counts; the
covariance is computed on the cells of type $j$ divided by their size
factor $L_c/\bar L_j$, with $L_c$ the full library of cell $c$ and
$\bar L_j$ its mean over the type. That removes the size term
$\nu\,\mu_g\mu_h$ and keeps the Poisson diagonal, which the archived
denoised covariance also removed. This is the size-normalised input of
the scale screen of chapter 4 (@sec-deconv-scale-screen).

Means and covariances are computed on the linear counts, never on
$\log(1+x)$: the convolution is linear only on an additive scale
([2.1, normalisation](https://bastienchassagnol.github.io/DeCovarT/articles/02-1-single-cell-generative-optimisation.html#sec-norm-linear)).
An earlier glasso script fitted and exported on $\log(1+x)$; that
choice was reverted before it was archived.

### Bulk depth per cell {#sec-cov-bulk-depth}

Which covariance enters the bulk law, raw or denoised, depends on the
bulk depth per cell relative to the reference
([3.2, raw or denoised](https://bastienchassagnol.github.io/DeCovarT/articles/03-2-covariance-network-estimation.html#sec-raw-vs-denoised)):
the raw covariance is exact only at $a_i=1$. For bulk sample $i$,

$$
a_i=\frac{y_{+i}/n_i}{\bar s_{\mathrm{ref}}},
$$ {#eq-cov-bulk-depth}

with $y_{+i}$ the total counts of the bulk sample, $n_i$ the
`r glossary("cell count", "number of cells")` in it, and

lowers the effective depth to $a_i/\omega_i$. The raw plug-in, the
implemented default, assumes $a_i=1$. $n_i$ is

## Estimators as run {#sec-cov-estimators}

Every arm keeps the sample variances and regularises the correlation
only. With $\boldsymbol{D}_j$ the sample variances and
$\boldsymbol{R}_j$ the sample correlation of type $j$ at one time,

$$
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}
=
\boldsymbol{D}_j^{1/2}\,\widetilde{\boldsymbol{R}}_j\,\boldsymbol{D}_j^{1/2},
$$ {#eq-cov-correlation-scale}

with $\widetilde{\boldsymbol{R}}_j$ one of the forms of
@tbl-cov-estimators. No arm penalises the diagonal; the reason is in
[3.2, diagonal](https://bastienchassagnol.github.io/DeCovarT/articles/03-2-covariance-network-estimation.html#sec-cov-diagonal).
`build_sigma()` builds @eq-cov-correlation-scale from the stored
moments. In the shrinkage arms a gene with no variance in a type gets
$10^{-4}$ times the median variance of that type. The factor arm uses
the scale of its own fit, the `r glossary("Poisson floor")` of
@sec-cov-cv-run, so that $\boldsymbol{B}_j\boldsymbol{B}_j^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_j$
maps back to the variances the factors were fitted on. The shrinkage
plug-in of the deconvolution is unchanged by that floor.

| Arm | $\widetilde{\boldsymbol{R}}_j$ | Tuning value | Grid | Free covariance parameters, $G$ genes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Diagonal | $\boldsymbol{I}$ | none ($\lambda=1$, or rank 0) | — | $G$ |
| Shrinkage | $(1-\lambda)\boldsymbol{R}_j+\lambda\boldsymbol{I}$ | $\lambda$, per type or one per time | 43 values: 13 log-spaced from $10^{-3}$ to 0.1, then 0.125 to 1 by $1/32$, and 0.9 | no count |
| Two-parameter | $(1-\lambda)\{(1-\alpha)\boldsymbol{R}_j+\alpha\bar{\boldsymbol{R}}\}+\lambda\boldsymbol{I}$ | $(\alpha,\lambda)$, per type or one pair per time | $\alpha\in\{0.25,0.5,0.75\}$ × the 43 levels | no count |
| Factor analysis, per type | $\boldsymbol{B}_j\boldsymbol{B}_j^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_j$ | rank $q_j$ | $\{0,1,2,3,5,8,12,20,30\}$ | $G+q_jG-q_j(q_j-1)/2$ |
| Factor analysis, shared loadings | $\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_j$ | rank $q$, one per time | $\{0,1,2,3,5,8,12,20\}$ | $JG+qG-q(q-1)/2$ for the $J$ types |

: The arms, on the correlation scale of @eq-cov-correlation-scale. $\bar{\boldsymbol{R}}$: pooled within-type correlation of the time. The grids are set in `scripts/03_04_cv_covariance.R`. {#tbl-cov-estimators}

### Shrinkage covariance {#sec-shrinkage}

$(1-\lambda)\boldsymbol{R}_j+\lambda\boldsymbol{I}$ is the
Schäfer–Strimmer target D: variances kept, every correlation multiplied
by $1-\lambda$ [@schäferShrinkageApproachLargescale2005]
[TODO-cite: Schäfer and Strimmer 2005, target D; p. to check]{.highlight}.
$\lambda$, the `r glossary("shrinkage intensity")`, is
chosen on held-out cells (@sec-cov-cv-run), either per type or as one
level common to the types of a time. The closed-form level is archived
(@sec-cov-archived). The estimate is positive definite for every
$\lambda>0$, whatever $C_j$ and $G_t$; the smallest level of the grid
is $10^{-3}$. The theory is in
[3.2, shrinkage](https://bastienchassagnol.github.io/DeCovarT/articles/03-2-covariance-network-estimation.html#sec-cov-shrinkage).

### Two-parameter shrinkage {#sec-cov-two-param}

$\bar{\boldsymbol{R}}$ is the correlation of
$\boldsymbol{W}=\sum_j (C_j-1)\boldsymbol{V}_j/(N-J)$, with
$\boldsymbol{V}_j$ the sample covariance of type $j$ and $N$ the cells
of the $J$ types of one time and one gene set
(`pooled_correlation()`). $\boldsymbol{W}$ is the tied covariance of
linear discriminant analysis. $\alpha$ moves $\boldsymbol{R}_j$ towards
$\bar{\boldsymbol{R}}$; $\lambda$ then moves the result towards
$\boldsymbol{I}$. This is the family of regularised discriminant
analysis
[TODO-cite: Friedman 1989, Regularized discriminant analysis, Journal of the American Statistical Association; p. to check]{.highlight}
[TODO-cite: Hastie, Tibshirani and Friedman 2009, The Elements of Statistical Learning, 2nd ed., Springer; ch. 4, sec. 4.3.1, p. 112, eqs. 4.13–4.14]{.highlight}.
In cross-validation $\bar{\boldsymbol{R}}$ never contains the held-out
cells of the type being scored.

$\bar{\boldsymbol{R}}$ alone is not an arm. It has $G_t(G_t-1)/2$ free
correlations, `r f1(min(pairs_m))` to `r f1(max(pairs_m))` million,
for `r big(min(cells_time))` to `r big(max(cells_time))` cells per time.
At 96 h it is singular: $\boldsymbol{W}$ has rank at most
$N-J$ = `r big(rank_w_96)`, for `r big(n_welch[["96h"]])` genes. It
enters only as a target, with $\alpha<1$ and $\lambda>0$.

### Factor analysis {#sec-cov-factor}

`fit_factor_correlation()` fits
$\boldsymbol{R}_j\approx\boldsymbol{B}_j\boldsymbol{B}_j^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_j$
with $q_j$ factors by maximum likelihood, with the EM steps of Rubin and
Thayer [TODO-cite: Rubin and Thayer 1982, EM algorithms for ML factor analysis, Psychometrika; p. to check]{.highlight}.
The columns of $\boldsymbol{B}_j$ are the
`r glossary("factor loading", "factor loadings")` and the diagonal
$\boldsymbol{\Psi}_j$ holds the `r glossary("uniqueness", "uniquenesses")`.
The steps are written on the cells, so the $G\times G$ correlation is
never formed. The cells are standardised with the Poisson floor of
@sec-cov-cv-run. The start is the probabilistic PCA solution; EM stops
after 200 steps. Uniquenesses are floored at 0.01: with fewer cells
than genes the likelihood is unbounded when one of them tends to zero.
Rank 0 is the diagonal model.

`fit_factor_shared()` fits
$\boldsymbol{R}_j\approx\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_j$:
the types of one time share the loadings and keep their own
uniquenesses ("same axes, own volumes"). It alternates closed-form
updates of the rows of $\boldsymbol{B}$ and of each
$\boldsymbol{\Psi}_j$, for at most 100 steps.

A factor model on $G$ genes has $G+qG-q(q-1)/2$ free covariance
parameters: loadings up to rotation, plus uniquenesses
[TODO-cite: Murphy 2012, Machine Learning: A Probabilistic Perspective, MIT Press; ch. 12, sec. 12.1.3, p. 384]{.highlight}.
Shared loadings are counted once, plus one uniqueness vector per type.
A rank is used only if it is at most the training cells minus two, at
most the rank of the training correlation, and leaves no more
parameters than the $G(G+1)/2$ entries of the matrix. Rank 30 is
therefore never fitted for the 29-cell somite differentiation front.

### Archived arms {#sec-cov-archived}

These arms are not run in the current pipeline. Their code and a
README are in `scripts/archives/sparse_precision_arms/` and
`scripts/archives/denoised_covariance/`.

**Sparse precisions.** The graphical lasso with an unpenalised
diagonal (`glasso`, EBIC path), the `SILGGM` support with a refit, and
`PLNnetwork`, a Poisson log-normal model with the full-library offset
$\log s_c$ and a latent precision $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_z^{-1}$
penalised off the diagonal:

$$
x_{gc}\mid\boldsymbol{z}_{\cdot c}\sim\mathrm{Poisson}\bigl(s_{c}e^{z_{gc}}\bigr),
\qquad
\boldsymbol{z}_{\cdot c}\sim\mathcal{N}_{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{z},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}).
$$ {#eq-pln}

None was run on the gated panel. The pipeline now regularises
covariances only; these precision estimators may return as a
sensitivity analysis. Theory:
[3.2, graphical lasso](https://bastienchassagnol.github.io/DeCovarT/articles/03-2-covariance-network-estimation.html#sec-cov-glasso),
[3.2, PLN](https://bastienchassagnol.github.io/DeCovarT/articles/03-2-covariance-network-estimation.html#sec-cov-pln).

- **Denoised covariance.** The Poisson diagonal and a share $\kappa$
  of the size term removed from the sample covariance, $\kappa$
  bracketed between 0 and $\hat\nu/(1+\hat\nu)$, with $\hat\nu$ from
  `extrinsic_noise_nu()`
  ([3.2, raw or denoised](https://bastienchassagnol.github.io/DeCovarT/articles/03-2-covariance-network-estimation.html#sec-raw-vs-denoised)).
  It gave the worst deconvolution of the second pass: RMSE
  `r f2(denoised_96)` at 96 h, Welch panel. These second-pass results
  are kept in `output/deconvolution/second_pass/`; why the input was
  dropped is in @sec-deconv-variants.
- **Closed-form shrinkage level.** The Schäfer–Strimmer
  $\hat\lambda^{\ast}$ of `corpcor` minimises a
  `r glossary("Frobenius risk")`, not the Gaussian likelihood that
  DeCovarT evaluates. In the first pass it was
  `r span(first_pass$lambda_star[first_pass$cells >= 3000], f3)` for the
  `r sum(first_pass$cells >= 3000)` units with
  `r big(min(first_pass$cells[first_pass$cells >= 3000]))` cells or more,
  and `r span(first_pass$lambda_star[first_pass$cells < 100])` for the

  (`output/deconvolution/second_pass/plugin_summary_first_pass.csv`).

## Cross-validation and outputs {#sec-cov-cv-run}

One loss selects every tuning value and compares the arms: the
`r glossary("held-out log-score", "held-out Gaussian log-score")` on
the linear scale. For held-out cells
$\mathcal{T}$ and an estimate
$(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}})$ fitted
without them,

$$
\ell(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}};\mathcal{T})
=
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{T}}
\log\phi_{G}\bigl(\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c};\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\bigr).
$$ {#eq-heldout-loglik}

Its expectation is maximised at the true mean and covariance whatever
the law of the cells (Stein's loss), so it is a legitimate loss even
though cells are not Gaussian
([3.2, Gaussian log-score](https://bastienchassagnol.github.io/DeCovarT/articles/03-2-covariance-network-estimation.html#prp-gaussian-logscore)).
Why it replaces AIC, BIC and EBIC as the selector is in
[3.2, cross-validation](https://bastienchassagnol.github.io/DeCovarT/articles/03-2-covariance-network-estimation.html#sec-cov-cv).
The bulk samples are never read.

**Folds.** Leave-one-out under 100 cells (the 36 and 66 naïve cells at
48 h and 72 h, the 29-cell somite front), 5-fold otherwise. The
two-parameter arm uses 5 folds in every unit. Shared loadings use 5
joint folds, each type split into 5. Folds split the cells of one unit:
the score measures generalisation to new cells of this specimen, not
to new specimens (@nte-pseudoreplication).

**Poisson floor.** `split_standardise()`
(`R/03_01_stats_covariance_estimation.R`) standardises the training and
test cells with the training mean $\bar x_g$ and standard deviation
$s_g$ of each gene, with the variance floored at its Poisson level plus
one count over the $C_{\mathrm{tr}}$ training cells:

$$
s_g^{2}=\max\Bigl(v_g,\ \bar x_g+\tfrac{1}{C_{\mathrm{tr}}}\Bigr),
$$ {#eq-cov-poisson-floor}

with $v_g$ the sample variance. A count cannot vary less than a Poisson
count; a smaller variance means too few cells, or the fractional counts
left by ambient-RNA correction. The floor replaced a defect found in the
shared-loadings arm. Before it, the held-out score of the 29-cell somite
differentiation front at 96 h (outer fold 2 of the nested comparison)
was about $-5\times10^{10}$. The gene *Fnbp1* has fractional
ambient-corrected counts in that type: training mean $4\times10^{-7}$,
standard deviation $1.9\times10^{-6}$. One count in a test cell then cost
about $6\times10^{11}$. With the floor that fold scores −9,260, and the
other types move by less than 0.3%. These values come from the
diagnosis of the defect, not from the tables below. The factor fits use
the same scale (`build_sigma()`, @sec-cov-estimators); the shrinkage
plug-in of the deconvolution is unchanged. The tables of
@sec-cov-results-discussion come from the rerun with the floor
(`r cv_date`).

**One spectral decomposition per training set.** The test cells are
standardised with the training means and the floored standard
deviations $s_g$ of @eq-cov-poisson-floor. Let
$\boldsymbol{R}=\sum_{i\le r}\ell_i\boldsymbol{v}_i\boldsymbol{v}_i^{\top}$
be the training correlation, of rank $r$ on $G$ genes. The eigenvectors
of $(1-\lambda)\boldsymbol{R}+\lambda\boldsymbol{I}$ do not depend on
$\lambda$; its eigenvalues are $e_i=(1-\lambda)\ell_i+\lambda$ on the
range and $\lambda$ on the null space. For a standardised test cell
$\boldsymbol{z}$,

$$
\log\phi
=
-\tfrac12\Bigl[
G\log 2\pi+2\sum_g\log s_g
+\sum_{i\le r}\log e_i+(G-r)\log\lambda
+\sum_{i\le r}\frac{(\boldsymbol{v}_i^{\top}\boldsymbol{z})^{2}}{e_i}
+\frac{\lVert\boldsymbol{z}\rVert^{2}-\sum_{i\le r}(\boldsymbol{v}_i^{\top}\boldsymbol{z})^{2}}{\lambda}
\Bigr].
$$ {#eq-cov-path-score}

<!-- chk: R1, R2 -->

One decomposition therefore scores the `r n_levels` levels
(`spectral_path_core()`). It is taken on the smaller of
$\boldsymbol{Z}\boldsymbol{Z}^{\top}$ and
$\boldsymbol{Z}^{\top}\boldsymbol{Z}$, with $\boldsymbol{Z}$ the
standardised training cells. The two-parameter arm needs one
decomposition per $\alpha$.

**Factor scores.** With
$\boldsymbol{M}=\boldsymbol{I}+\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{-1}\boldsymbol{B}$,
of size $q\times q$,

$$
(\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi})^{-1}
=
\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{-1}-\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{-1}\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{M}^{-1}\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Psi}^{-1},
\qquad
\det(\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}+\boldsymbol{\Psi})
=
\det\boldsymbol{\Psi}\,\det\boldsymbol{M}
$$ {#eq-cov-woodbury}

<!-- chk: R3 -->

(`factor_core()`). No $G\times G$ matrix is factorised.

**Constant genes.** A gene constant in a training set is scored apart,
as an independent Gaussian with the variance of
@eq-cov-poisson-floor. That part of the score is the same for every
tuning value; it is left out of the fold-to-fold spread.

**Rules.**

- *Per type:* the value with the highest mean held-out score (`min`).
  The one-standard-error choice, the most regularised value within one
  standard error of that maximum, is reported beside it (`1se`).
- *Common:* one value per time, the one with the highest sum over types
  of the mean held-out score. For shared loadings this is the only rule.

**Nested comparison.** A `r glossary("nested cross-validation")`: the
cells of each time are split into 5 outer folds, stratified by type. Each rule chooses its tuning value on the
outer training cells only, with the folds above, and is scored on the
outer test cells. Scores are paired by fold and type; the gain is
reported against the diagonal model.

**Information criteria.** The factor models are refitted on all cells
of each unit (stage `fit`; these fits enter the deconvolution). AIC
and BIC are computed from the free-parameter counts of
@tbl-cov-estimators, plus $G$ means per type. They are reported, not
used to select.

**Grid search only.** Every tuning value is chosen on a fixed grid.
With one or two tuning parameters a grid is as reliable as random
search, whose advantage appears when few of many hyperparameters matter
[TODO-cite: Bergstra and Bengio 2012, Random search for
hyper-parameter optimization, Journal of Machine Learning Research
13:281–305; p. 283]{.highlight}. Here the shrinkage "path" is itself a
grid, of `r n_levels` levels, scored from one spectral decomposition
per fold (@eq-cov-path-score); the two-parameter arm adds one
decomposition per $\alpha$. The former `search` stage compared, on 5
folds, the exact path on 200 levels, a 10-value grid with one Cholesky
factor per value, `optimize()` on the path over $[10^{-3},1]$ and 10
random levels; for the two-parameter arm, on panels of at most 1,500
genes, a $5\times8$ grid in $(\alpha,\lambda)$ against 40 random pairs.
It is archived in `scripts/archives/search_comparison/` (see its
README); its table, `output/grn/cv/search_comparison.csv`, is still read
here (@sec-cov-search-results). The loss of a strategy is the held-out
score of the best path value minus that of its choice.

```pseudocode
#| label: algo-cv-covariance
#| html-line-number: true
#| pdf-line-number: true

\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Held-out scores along the paths, selection, and nested comparison}
\begin{algorithmic}
\Procedure{PathScores}{$X_{\mathrm{train}}, X_{\mathrm{test}}, \Lambda, Q, A$}
  \State standardise both sets with the training means and standard deviations
  \State $(\ell, V) \gets$ non-zero spectrum of the training correlation $R$
  \For{$\lambda \in \Lambda$}
    \State $s(\lambda) \gets$ mean path score of the test cells, from $(\ell, V)$
  \EndFor
  \For{$q \in Q$}
    \State $(B, \Psi) \gets$ \Call{FactorEM}{$X_{\mathrm{train}}, q$}, started from $(\ell, V)$
    \State $s(q) \gets$ mean test score through the Woodbury identity
  \EndFor
  \For{$\alpha \in A$}
    \State spectrum of $(1-\alpha)R + \alpha\bar R$; $s(\alpha,\lambda)$ for every $\lambda \in \Lambda$
  \EndFor
  \State \Return $s$
\EndProcedure
\Procedure{Select}{$X$, folds}
  \For{each fold $k$}
    \State $s_k \gets$ \Call{PathScores}{$X_{-k}, X_k, \Lambda, Q, A$}
  \EndFor
  \State $m \gets$ mean of $s_k$ over folds
  \State \Return $\arg\max m$ per type; $\arg\max \sum_j m_j$ for a common value
\EndProcedure
\Procedure{NestedCompare}{$X_1,\ldots,X_J$}
  \State split each $X_j$ into 5 outer folds
  \For{$k = 1,\ldots,5$}
    \For{each rule $e$}
      \State choose its value by \Call{Select}{} on the outer training cells
      \State $g_{ek} \gets$ test score of rule $e$ $-$ test score of the diagonal
    \EndFor
  \EndFor
  \State \Return $(g_{ek})$, paired by fold
\EndProcedure
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
```

## Results and discussion {#sec-cov-results-discussion}

### Regularisation shrinks covariance terms without setting them to zero {#sec-cov-shrinkage-reading}

**What the shrinkage arm returns.** The estimate is
$\boldsymbol{D}_j^{1/2}\{(1-\lambda)\boldsymbol{R}_j+\lambda\boldsymbol{I}\}\boldsymbol{D}_j^{1/2}$
(@sec-shrinkage). Three readings follow.

- **Variances are untouched.** Only the correlations move.
- **Every correlation is multiplied by $1-\lambda$; none becomes
  zero.** The estimate has as many non-zero off-diagonal terms as the
  sample covariance: all $G_t(G_t-1)/2$ pairs, that is
  `r and_list(paste(f1(pairs_m), "million at", hours(names(pairs_m))))`
  on the Welch panel. Its inverse is dense as well, so no graph can be read from
  either matrix. The factor models are dense too: each off-diagonal
  term is an entry of $\boldsymbol{B}_j\boldsymbol{B}_j^{\top}$.
- **$\lambda$ is a floor on the spectrum.** Each eigenvalue $\ell$ of
  $\boldsymbol{R}_j$ becomes $(1-\lambda)\ell+\lambda$, so the smallest
  eigenvalue of the shrunk correlation is at least $\lambda$. That
  floor makes the estimate positive definite when $C_j\le G_t$.

**A dense estimate is not evidence that every pair of genes
interacts.** Three mechanisms give a non-zero covariance to a pair
with no direct link.

- **Sampling noise.** A sample correlation is never exactly zero, even
  for independent genes. Shrinkage multiplies it by $1-\lambda$; it
  does not threshold.
- **Covariance is not the network.** Direct interactions are the zeros
  of the precision matrix. Two genes linked only through other genes
  still have a non-zero covariance. A covariance block is zero only
  between groups of genes with no path between them. Isolated groups
  could be looked for with the archived sparse arms
  (@sec-cov-archived), not with these.
- **Raw counts share a cell-size factor.** The covariances are computed
  on raw counts (@sec-cov-inputs), so every pair of genes receives the
  size term $\nu\,\mu_g\mu_h$, positive for all pairs. This is the
  extrinsic noise that the archived `extrinsic_noise_nu()` estimated.

### Information criteria {#sec-cov-ic-results}

On the Welch panel (`information_criteria.csv`), AIC chooses the
largest fitted rank for `r sum(ic_type$aic == ic_type$top)` of
`r nrow(ic_type)` types, and rank
`r and_list(paste(ic_shared$aic, "at", hours(ic_shared$time)))` for the
shared loadings. BIC chooses ranks `r min(ic_type$bic)` to 8 for
`r sum(ic_type$bic <= 8)` types`r if (nrow(bic_high)) paste0(", and ", and_list(paste(bic_high$bic, "for", unit_the(bic_high$time, bic_high$cell_type))))`.
For the shared loadings BIC chooses
`r and_list(paste(ic_shared$bic, "at", hours(ic_shared$time)))`. Of the
`r sum(ic$arm == "factor")` per-type fits on all cells,
`r sum(ic$arm == "factor" & !ic$converged)` stopped at the 200-step
limit; `r sum(ic$arm != "factor" & ic$converged)` of the
`r sum(ic$arm != "factor")` shared fits converged.

### What this implies for the deconvolution {#sec-cov-deconv-implication}

The acceptance of a dense plug-in (@sec-cov-scope) concerns the
likelihood, not the interpretation. The levels that predict held-out
cells best are too strong for the bulk (@sec-deconv-selection). On the
Welch panel at 96 h the `r glossary("RMSE")` of the proportions is
`r f3(rmse_id("cv_shrinkage_per_type"))` with the per-type levels and
`r f3(rmse_id("cv_shrinkage_common"))` with the common level, against
`r f3(bulk_pick$rmse)` at $\lambda=`r bulk_pick$tuning`$, the level
chosen by the bulk likelihood (`output/deconvolution/lambda_selection.csv`).
