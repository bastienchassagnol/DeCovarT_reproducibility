4  Covariance estimation

4.1 Scope and status

This chapter estimates, for each scored cell type j at each bulk-matched time, the mean \widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j} and the within-type covariance \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} of the gene panel of Section 3.6, and exports them on the linear scale of DeCovarT’s convolution,

\bigl\{ \widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j},\, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j},\, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j}=\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}^{-1} \bigr\}_{j=1}^{J}, \qquad \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}\succ 0 . \tag{4.1}

The target is the covariance that enters the likelihood, not a mechanistic gene network; a dense estimate is therefore an acceptable plug-in and is the default (3.2, target).

4.1.1 Units and regimes

Each fit uses the cells of one type at one time, treated as independent draws: eleven type × time units (Table 3.2). Cell line is not a covariate (Section 2.1). The panel has G=500 genes per time before genes constant in a type are removed.

In the seven units with C_j<G the sample covariance is singular, and only a regularised estimate exists. Which thresholds matter for existence, for the maximum-likelihood estimate and for support recovery is in 3.2, regimes. The cross-validated score of Section 4.3 is defined in every regime because each estimator returns a positive definite matrix.

4.1.2 Inputs and scales

All four estimators read data/intermediate/equal_type_top500_by_time.rds (Section 3.6). A transformation belongs to one estimator only.

Two earlier choices are reverted, both in code that has not been re-run:

  • Scale of the export. scripts/03_01_grn_huge.R used to fit and export on \log(1+x) (x <- log1p(x), then mu <- colMeans(x)). The convolution is linear only on an additive scale (2.1, normalisation), so \widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j} and \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} are now computed on size-factor-normalised counts. A \log(1+x) table may still be used to detect a support, never to export a moment.
  • Library size in PLNnetwork. scripts/03_03_grn_plnnetwork.R used the row sum over the 500 panel genes as offset. It now uses the full RNA library size, recovered from log_umi (s_c=10^{\texttt{log\_umi}}), as in Equation 3.5. nCount_RNA in the exported object is the 500-gene sum and is not used.

Pearson residuals, of the legacy SCTransform fit (Section 3.9.1) or of the count GLMs of the new rank (Section 3.3.1), are used only for ranking and are never passed to an estimator.

4.2 Four estimators as run

All four leave the diagonal of the precision unpenalised. Why the diagonal must not be penalised is in 3.2, diagonal. Each estimator has exactly one tuning parameter, chosen by Section 4.3.

4.2.1 Shrinkage covariance (default)

fit_cov_shrinkage() calls corpcor::cov.shrink() (Schäfer et al. 2021) with the Schäfer–Strimmer target D: variances kept, correlations shrunk towards zero by a common factor (Schäfer and Strimmer 2005). corpcor 1.6.10 also shrinks the variances towards their median by default; lambda.var = 0 switches that off, so the target is exactly D. The closed-form intensity \hat\lambda^{\ast} (corpcor::estimate.lambda()) is the centre of the grid, and cross-validation picks the value used. The estimate is positive definite for any C_j and G, so it is the estimator used when the others fail. The theory is in 3.2, shrinkage. corpcor is not in renv.lock.

4.2.2 Graphical lasso with an unpenalised diagonal

fit_cov_glasso() standardises the panel, fits glasso::glasso(R, rho = lambda, penalize.diagonal = FALSE) (Friedman et al. 2019) on the sample correlation \boldsymbol{R}_j by block coordinate descent (Friedman et al. 2008), and rescales: \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j}=\Delta_j^{-1/2}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{R}\Delta_j^{-1/2} with \Delta_j=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{V}_j) (rescale_correlation_fit()). With the diagonal unpenalised the fitted correlation keeps a unit diagonal, so \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} matches the sample variances.

  • penalize.diagonal defaults to TRUE in glasso 1.11; it is set explicitly.
  • huge (Zhao et al. 2022) is not used: huge.glasso() always penalises the diagonal and has no switch for it (checked in the huge 1.5.1 sources). The reasons are in 3.3, graphical lasso.
  • Grid endpoints come from lambda_endpoints(): at \lambda_{\max}=\max_{g\neq g'}|R_{gg'}| the off-diagonal precision is zero; the lower end is of order \sqrt{\log G/C_j}.
  • A gene constant across the cells of a type is left out of that type’s fit and listed in excluded_genes, instead of forcing a diagonal covariance for all genes.
  • A penalty whose solve fails is dropped from the grid and listed in failed_lambda, instead of being replaced by a diagonal precision.

The earlier selector, a safeguarded EBIC interval search (ebic_adaptive_path(), at most n_solves = 40 solves, \gamma=0.5 (Foygel and Drton 2010)), is kept in scripts/03_01_grn_huge.R as a diagnostic path. It no longer chooses \lambda.

4.2.3 SILGGM support, then a refit

fit_cov_silggm_refit() runs in three steps:

  1. Nonparanormal scores with huge::huge.npn(x, npn.func = "shrinkage") (rank R_{gc} mapped to \Phi^{-1}\{R_{gc}/(C_j+1)\}, then standardised). The choice of shrinkage over truncation is argued in 3.3, nonparanormal.
  2. SILGGM::SILGGM(npn, method = "D-S_GL", global = TRUE, alpha) (Zhang et al. 2018b, 2018a): de-sparsified graphical lasso with the global FDR rule of
    1. The object kept is the support at level \alpha (global_decision), not the dense de-sparsified matrix.
  3. An SPD refit on the linear-scale panel under the zeros of that support, with glasso::glasso(V, rho = 0, zero = ..., penalize.diagonal = FALSE).

The script scripts/03_02_grn_silggm.R (seed 1, \alpha=0.05) stops after step 2 and has never been run; the refit lives in the wrapper.

4.2.4 PLNnetwork

fit_cov_plnnetwork() fits the Poisson-lognormal network of PLNmodels (Chiquet et al. 2025, 2018) to the raw counts of one unit, with the full-library offset:

x_{gc}\mid\boldsymbol{z}_{\cdot c} \sim \mathrm{Poisson}\bigl(s_{c}\,e^{z_{gc}}\bigr), \qquad \boldsymbol{z}_{\cdot c} \sim \mathcal{N}_{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{z},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}), \qquad \boldsymbol{\Omega}_{z}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}^{-1}\ \text{penalised off the diagonal}. \tag{4.2}

\begin{algorithm} \caption{PLNnetwork: alternating graphical lasso and variational updates} \begin{algorithmic} \Require counts $X$, offsets $O=\log s$, penalty $\lambda$, weights $W$ \State initialise the variational means $M$, variances $S$ and $\Sigma$ \For{$h = 1,\ldots,$ maxit\_out} \State $\Omega^{(h)} \gets$ \Call{GraphicalLasso}{$\Sigma^{(h-1)}, \lambda W$} \State $(B^{(h)},M^{(h)},S^{(h)}) \gets$ \Call{NLopt}{$B^{(h-1)},M^{(h-1)},S^{(h-1)};\Omega^{(h)}$} \State $\Sigma^{(h)} \gets C^{-1}\left[{M^{(h)}}^\top M^{(h)} + \sum_c S_c^{(h)}\right]$ \State $F_h \gets -\mathrm{ELBO}(B^{(h)},M^{(h)},S^{(h)},\Omega^{(h)}) + \lambda\|\Omega^{(h)}\|_{1,W}$ \If{$|F_h-F_{h-1}|/|F_h| <$ ftol\_out} \State \textbf{break} \EndIf \EndFor \State \Return $(B,M,S,\Omega,\Sigma)$ \end{algorithmic} \end{algorithm}

Algorithm 4.1 is the block-coordinate scheme of Chiquet et al. (2018). The model, the variational bound and the role of the offset are in 3.3, PLN.

The latent moments (\boldsymbol{\mu}_{z},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}) are not on DeCovarT’s scale. pln_linear_moments() maps them to the moments of the counts, averaged over the size factors s_c of the cells being scored:

\mathbb{E}\,x_{gc}=s_{c}\,e^{\mu_{z,g}+\sigma_{z,gg}/2}, \qquad \operatorname{Cov}\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c} = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbb{E}\,\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c}) + \bigl(\mathbb{E}\,\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c}\,\mathbb{E}\,\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c}^{\top}\bigr) \circ \bigl(e^{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}}-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bigr), \tag{4.3}

with e^{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}} taken entrywise. The moments are then divided by the size factor, so that the four estimators are scored on the same size-factor-normalised table.

4.3 Cross-validation and outputs

The same loss selects the tuning parameter of every estimator and compares the four: the held-out Gaussian log-likelihood on the linear scale. For held-out cells \mathcal{T} and an estimate (\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}) fitted without them,

\ell(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}};\mathcal{T}) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{T}} \log\phi_{G}\bigl(\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c};\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\bigr), \tag{4.4}

computed by heldout_gaussian_logscore() through a Cholesky factor of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}. Why this score is a legitimate loss for the covariance even though cells are not Gaussian, and why it replaces AIC, BIC and EBIC as the selector, is in 3.2, cross-validation.

\begin{algorithm} \caption{Tuning by K-fold cross-validation, and nested comparison of the four estimators} \begin{algorithmic} \Procedure{CVSelect}{$X, f, \Lambda, K$} \State split the cells of $X$ into $K$ folds at random, with a fixed seed \For{$\lambda \in \Lambda$} \For{$k = 1,\ldots,K$} \State $(\hat\mu,\hat\Sigma) \gets f(X_{-k};\lambda)$ \State $\ell_k(\lambda) \gets$ \Call{HeldOutLogScore}{$\hat\mu,\hat\Sigma,X_k$} \EndFor \State $m(\lambda) \gets$ mean of $\ell_k(\lambda)$; $\mathrm{se}(\lambda) \gets$ its standard error \EndFor \State $\lambda_{\min} \gets \arg\max_{\lambda} m(\lambda)$ \State $\lambda_{1\mathrm{se}} \gets$ most regularised $\lambda$ with $m(\lambda) \ge m(\lambda_{\min}) - \mathrm{se}(\lambda_{\min})$ \State \Return $(m, \mathrm{se}, \lambda_{\min}, \lambda_{1\mathrm{se}})$ \EndProcedure \Procedure{NestedCompare}{$X, \{(f_e,\Lambda_e)\}_{e=1}^{4}, K_{\mathrm{out}}, K_{\mathrm{in}}$} \For{$k = 1,\ldots,K_{\mathrm{out}}$} \For{$e = 1,\ldots,4$} \State $\lambda_e \gets \lambda_{\min}$ of \Call{CVSelect}{$X_{-k}, f_e, \Lambda_e, K_{\mathrm{in}}$} \State $(\hat\mu,\hat\Sigma) \gets f_e(X_{-k};\lambda_e)$ \State $s_{ek} \gets$ \Call{HeldOutLogScore}{$\hat\mu,\hat\Sigma,X_k$} \EndFor \EndFor \State \Return $(s_{ek})$, paired by outer fold \EndProcedure \end{algorithmic} \end{algorithm}

Settings in scripts/03_04_cv_covariance.R:

  • cv_select_tuning(X, fit_fun, grid, K = 5, seed) returns the mean and standard error per grid value and the min and 1se choices. The exported fit uses min; 1se is reported beside it.
  • nested_cv_compare(X, estimators, K_outer = 5, K_inner = 5, seed) is run only to compare the four estimators. The inner loop chooses the tuning parameter, the outer loop scores the chosen fit on cells it never saw.
  • Folds split cells of one unit. The score measures generalisation to new cells of this specimen, not to new specimens (Note 3.1).
  • In units with very few cells (29 cells for the somite differentiation front) each training fold has about 23 cells; the shrinkage estimate is the only one expected to be stable there.

Diagnostics that are computed but do not select: the EBIC path of the graphical lasso (Section 4.2.2), and edge stability under subsampling. StARS is not run: with 20 subsamples and 40 penalties it costs about 800 graphical-lasso solves per unit, against 40 for the EBIC path.

For the estimator retained for a unit, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j} is the mean of the size-factor-normalised counts of its cells and \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} is the selected covariance, both on the scale of Section 4.1.2; Equation 4.1 holds by construction. For PLNnetwork both come from Equation 4.3. Which covariance layer these cell-level moments identify, and how the sample scales are estimated inside the DeCovarT fit, is in 2.1, three regimes and 2.1, profile Newton.

4.3.1 Displays

Plot functions are in R/plot_grn_regularisation.R. Each display answers one question.

Choosing the tuning parameter (one unit, one estimator).

  • Cross-validation curve: m(\lambda)\pm\mathrm{se}(\lambda) against the tuning parameter, with the min and 1se choices marked (new; not produced yet).
  • Regularisation path: each off-diagonal entry of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}(\lambda) against \log\lambda, five edges that survive at the selected \lambda and five that are driven to zero (graphical lasso, PLNnetwork).
  • EBIC against \log\lambda, and EBIC against edge density |E|/\{G(G-1)/2\} (diagnostic only).

Comparing estimators (one unit).

  • Outer-fold held-out scores s_{ek} of Algorithm 4.2, one line per fold across the four estimators, so that the comparison is paired.
  • Partial-correlation heatmaps at the selected tuning parameter, with one gene order frozen across estimators and types, \rho_{gg'\mid\mathrm{rest}} = -\frac{\omega_{gg'}}{\sqrt{\omega_{gg}\omega_{g'g'}}}, \tag{4.5} on a diverging scale centred at zero.
  • For the three sparse estimators: UpSet plots of edge presence and of signed edges.

Comparing types (one time). Pairwise Hellinger distance and MixSim overlap between the exported Gaussians on the linear scale (gaussian_hellinger(), gaussian_mixsim_overlap() in R/ebic_regularisation_path.R). Distances computed on another scale (nonparanormal scores, \log(1+x)) do not describe what DeCovarT has to separate.

4.3.2 Evaluation

There is no ground-truth network for murine gastruloids, and the estimand is a covariance. The scores are therefore relative, in this order of weight:

  1. Held-out likelihood, outer folds of Algorithm 4.2: the selector, and the only score that compares all four estimators on one scale.
  2. Downstream deconvolution: DeCovarT error on the matched 48 h bulk and in the misspecification sweep of Section 7.1, with \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} from each estimator. This is the score tied to the paper’s estimand.
  3. Technical stability: a bootstrap of cells within type, labels fixed (Note 3.1). Edge-inclusion frequencies for the sparse estimators and the Frobenius spread of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} for all four. Conditional on this specimen.
  4. Separation of types: Hellinger and MixSim overlap of
  5. Concordance of the sparse estimators: overlap of edge sets (intersection over the smaller set) and Hamming distance between skeletons, raw and divided by G(G-1)/2. Agreement is a robustness number, not a validation.

Scores that need a known skeleton, curated transcription-factor targets, other assays or perturbations are perspectives (3.3, perspectives).

Note 4.1: ℹ️ No zero-inflation layer

Suppinger is droplet UMI data. Zeros are modelled as Poisson or negative-binomial sampling zeros (Svensson 2020); no estimator in this book has a zero-inflation or hurdle layer. Zero-inflated observation models appear only as a contamination in Section 7.1.
