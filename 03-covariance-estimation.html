4.1 Scope and status

This chapter estimates, for each scored cell type j at each bulk-matched time, the mean \widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j} and the within-type covariance \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} of the gene panel of tier (ii) (Section 3.3), and exports them on the linear scale of DeCovarT’s convolution,

\bigl\{ \widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j},\, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j},\, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j}=\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}^{-1} \bigr\}_{j=1}^{J}, \qquad \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}\succ 0 . \tag{4.1}

The target is the covariance that enters the likelihood, not a mechanistic gene network; a dense estimate is therefore an acceptable plug-in (3.2, why a regularised covariance).

Plug-in and sensitivity analyses. The exported plug-in is the shrinkage covariance (Section 4.2.1). Four arms are sensitivity analyses of that choice: the graphical lasso (3), the SILGGM support with a refit (Section 4.2.4), PLNnetwork (Section 4.2.5) and the shrinkage of the denoised covariance (2). All five are scored by the same nested cross-validation (Section 4.3); their theory is summarised in 3.2, estimators.

4.1.1 Inputs and scales

Every arm reads the raw UMI counts of the scored cells, element slots[[time]] of output/feature_selection/tier_01/scored_slots.rds (Section 3.2.1): counts (genes × cells), cell_type, batch and library_size, the full RNA library of each cell. The counts are restricted to the genes of output/feature_selection/tier_02/selected_<time>.csv, the panel gated by the permuted Welch G at BH 0.05 (Section 3.3): 1,417 genes at 48 h, 3,420 at 72 h and 5,821 at 96 h, with no cap. read_panel_slot() does the subset and returns cells × genes. The 500-gene legacy export of Section 3.6 is no longer read. A transformation belongs to one arm only.

Normalisation. The exported means and covariances are computed on raw per-cell UMI counts, with no per-cell library division (normalise = "none"). Dividing each cell by its total library removes the type-level transcriptome size S_j: signatures become mRNA fractions, and the proportions are estimated as q_j\propto p_jS_j instead of p_j (Lu et al. 2025). It also removes the biological size variation between cells of one type, which the bulk accumulates. Without spike-ins the technical size factor cannot be separated from the biological one, so the size term of the covariance is bracketed by the coefficient \kappa of the denoised estimator (2): \kappa=0 treats all size variation as biological; \kappa=\hat\nu/(1+\hat\nu) treats it all as technical, which matches library normalisation for the covariance. The argument is in 3.2, raw or denoised. The earlier per-cell division, X <- y / (lib / ref_lib) with ref_lib the median library of the time point, is kept only as a sensitivity variant (normalise = "library"). PLNnetwork keeps its full-library offset \log s_c in both cases.

Earlier choices reverted, in code that has not been re-run:

  • Scale of the export. scripts/03_01_grn_glasso.R (then named 03_01_grn_huge.R) used to fit and export on \log(1+x) (x <- log1p(x), then mu <- colMeans(x)). The convolution is linear only on an additive scale (2.1, normalisation), so \widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j} and \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} are now computed on the linear counts. A \log(1+x) table may still be used to detect a support, never to export a moment.
  • Per-cell library division. scripts/03_04_cv_covariance.R divided each cell by its library (X <- y / (lib / ref_lib)), which is option (b) of 3.2, raw or denoised. It is now the normalise = "library" variant.

4.1.2 Units and regimes

Each fit uses the cells of one type at one time, treated as independent draws: eleven type × time units (Table 3.2). Cell line is not a covariate (Section 2.1). The panel has G_t genes at time t, before genes constant within a type are removed. Table 4.3 sets the C_j cells of each unit against three sample-size layers (3.2, regimes): existence of the maximum-likelihood estimate of \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}, support recovery by the graphical lasso, and support recovery by neighbourhood selection.

In nine of the eleven units C_j\le G_t: the sample covariance is singular, and only a regularised estimate exists. Only the 48 h epiblast (10,590 cells against 1,417 genes) and the 72 h primitive streak (4,279 against 3,420) have a maximum-likelihood estimate. Four units exceed the graphical-lasso order and eight the neighbourhood-selection order; the 48 h and 72 h naïve pluripotency and the somite differentiation front exceed neither. The cross-validated score of Section 4.3 is defined in every regime because each arm returns a positive definite matrix.

4.1.3 Bulk depth per cell

Which covariance enters the bulk law, raw or denoised, depends on the bulk depth per cell relative to the reference (3.2, raw or denoised): the raw covariance is exact only at a_i=1. For bulk sample i,

a_i=\frac{y_{+i}/n_i}{\bar s_{\mathrm{ref}}}, \tag{4.2}

with y_{+i} the total counts of the bulk sample, n_i the number of cells in it, and \bar s_{\mathrm{ref}} the median UMIs per reference cell at the matched time. a_i<1 is expected: the bulk library is deeper in total than one droplet, but it spreads that depth over every cell of the pooled sample. Bulk counts are reads, not UMIs, so their sampling noise is overdispersed, with a dispersion \omega_i\ge1 that lowers the effective depth to a_i/\omega_i. The raw plug-in, the implemented default, assumes a_i=1. n_i is TODO: cells per gastruloid × gastruloids per bulk sample, from the Suppinger et al. 2023 methods (Suppinger et al. 2023).

4.2 Estimators as run

None of the five arms penalises the diagonal: the three sparse estimators leave the diagonal of the precision unpenalised, and the shrinkage target D keeps the variances. Why the diagonal must not be penalised is in 3.2, diagonal. Each arm has exactly one tuning parameter, chosen by Section 4.3.

4.2.1 Shrinkage covariance

fit_cov_shrinkage() calls corpcor::cov.shrink() (Schäfer et al. 2021) with the Schäfer–Strimmer target D: variances kept, correlations shrunk towards zero by a common factor (Schäfer and Strimmer 2005). corpcor 1.6.10 also shrinks the variances towards their median by default; lambda.var = 0 switches that off, so the target is exactly D. The closed-form intensity \hat\lambda^{\ast} (corpcor::estimate.lambda()) is the centre of the grid, and cross-validation picks the value used. The estimate is positive definite for any C_j and G_t, so it is the plug-in, and the estimator used when the others fail. The theory is in 3.2, shrinkage.

4.2.2 Denoised shrinkage

fit_cov_shrinkage(preprocess = "denoised", kappa) applies the moment correction of 3.2, raw or denoised before the shrinkage. With \boldsymbol{V}_j the unbiased sample covariance of the raw counts of the C_j cells and \bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_j their mean (denoise_covariance()),

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{V}}_j = \boldsymbol{V}_j-\operatorname{diag}(\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_j) -\kappa\Bigl[\boldsymbol{V}_j-\operatorname{diag}(\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_j) +\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_j\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}_j^{\top} -\frac{\boldsymbol{V}_j}{C_j}\Bigr]. \tag{4.3}

  1. The eigenvalues of \widetilde{\boldsymbol{V}}_j below zero are raised to zero, the nearest positive semi-definite matrix in Frobenius norm TODO-cite: Datta & Zou 2017 (CoCoLasso); Loh & Wainwright 2012; keys not in bibliography/REFERENCES.bib. This costs one eigen-decomposition of a G_t\times G_t matrix per training set.
  2. A gene whose denoised variance is not positive is a Poisson gene at this depth. Its variance is floored at 10^{-4} times the median positive denoised variance, its correlations are set to zero, and it is listed in floored_genes.
  3. Target D is applied to the projected matrix: \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\mathrm{bio}}_{j}=\boldsymbol{D}^{1/2}\{(1-\lambda)\boldsymbol{R}+\lambda\boldsymbol{I}\}\boldsymbol{D}^{1/2}, with \boldsymbol{R} and \boldsymbol{D} its correlation and variances, and \lambda chosen on the grid of Section 4.2.1.

The exported matrix is \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\mathrm{bio}}_{j}. The held-out cells are raw counts and carry the Poisson noise and the size term that the correction removed, so the arm is scored through the raw count covariance rebuilt from the estimate, with \nu^{\mathrm{tech}}=\kappa/(1-\kappa):

\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\mathrm{score}}_{j} = \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\mathrm{bio}}_{j} +\operatorname{diag}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j}) +\frac{\kappa}{1-\kappa} \bigl(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\mathrm{bio}}_{j} +\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j}^{\top}\bigr) \tag{4.4}

(reconstruct_count_covariance(), element score_sigma of the fit, used by score_fit() when present). With \kappa=0 it is \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\mathrm{bio}}_{j}+\operatorname{diag}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j}). The cross-validation therefore compares only how the biological part is regularised; it cannot choose \kappa, because every \kappa rebuilds the same raw moments up to regularisation. \kappa stays a bracket: the nested comparison uses \kappa=0, which needs no estimate, and the upper end \hat\kappa_{\max}=\hat\nu/(1+\hat\nu) is exported as a variant (shrinkage_denoised_kappa_max), tuned but not compared.

\hat\nu comes from extrinsic_noise_nu(): the mode of the normalised covariances \widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(x_g,x_h)/(\bar x_g\bar x_h) over pairs of genes with a mean of at least one count (at most 2,000 genes, largest means first), after Fang and Pachter TODO-cite: Fang & Pachter 2025, extrinsic biological stochasticity; key fangExtrinsicBiologicalStochasticity2025 is in the DeCovarT bibliography, not in bibliography/REFERENCES.bib. The estimate assumes that most gene pairs are intrinsically uncorrelated; co-regulated modules bias \hat\nu upwards. Without spike-ins \hat\nu mixes the biological and technical size factors, so \hat\kappa_{\max} is the all-technical end of the bracket. The estimates are written per unit to output/grn/cv/extrinsic_noise.csv.

The graphical lasso and the SILGGM refit accept the same preprocessing (preprocess = "denoised"). The graphical lasso then fits the correlation of the projected matrix. For SILGGM, ranks cannot be denoised: the support still comes from the nonparanormal scores, and only the linear-scale refit on that support uses the projected matrix. Neither variant is an arm of the nested comparison.

4.2.3 Graphical lasso with an unpenalised diagonal

fit_cov_glasso() standardises the panel, fits glasso::glasso(R, rho = lambda, penalize.diagonal = FALSE) (Friedman et al. 2019) on the sample correlation \boldsymbol{R}_j by block coordinate descent (Friedman et al. 2008), and rescales: \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{j}=\Delta_j^{-1/2}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Omega}}_{R}\Delta_j^{-1/2} with \Delta_j=\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{V}_j) (rescale_correlation_fit()). With the diagonal unpenalised the fitted correlation keeps a unit diagonal, so \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} matches the sample variances.

  • penalize.diagonal defaults to TRUE in glasso 1.11; it is set explicitly.
  • huge (Zhao et al. 2022) is not used: huge.glasso() always penalises the diagonal and has no switch for it (checked in the huge 1.5.1 sources). The reasons are in 3.2, graphical lasso.
  • Grid endpoints come from lambda_endpoints(): at \lambda_{\max}=\max_{g\neq g'}|R_{gg'}| the off-diagonal precision is zero; the lower end is of order \sqrt{\log G_t/C_j}.
  • A gene constant across the cells of a type is left out of that type’s fit and listed in excluded_genes, instead of forcing a diagonal covariance for all genes.
  • A penalty whose solve fails is dropped from the grid and listed in failed_lambda, instead of being replaced by a diagonal precision.

The earlier selector, a safeguarded EBIC interval search (ebic_adaptive_path(), at most n_solves = 40 solves, \gamma=0.5 (Foygel and Drton 2010)), is kept in scripts/03_01_grn_glasso.R as a diagnostic path. It no longer chooses \lambda.

4.2.5 PLNnetwork

fit_cov_plnnetwork() fits the Poisson-lognormal network of PLNmodels (Chiquet et al. 2025, 2018) to the raw counts of one unit, with the full-library offset:

x_{gc}\mid\boldsymbol{z}_{\cdot c} \sim \mathrm{Poisson}\bigl(s_{c}\,e^{z_{gc}}\bigr), \qquad \boldsymbol{z}_{\cdot c} \sim \mathcal{N}_{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{z},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}), \qquad \boldsymbol{\Omega}_{z}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}^{-1}\ \text{penalised off the diagonal}. \tag{4.5}

\begin{algorithm} \caption{PLNnetwork: alternating graphical lasso and variational updates} \begin{algorithmic} \Require counts $X$, offsets $O=\log s$, penalty $\lambda$, weights $W$ \State initialise the variational means $M$, variances $S$ and $\Sigma$ \For{$h = 1,\ldots,$ maxit\_out} \State $\Omega^{(h)} \gets$ \Call{GraphicalLasso}{$\Sigma^{(h-1)}, \lambda W$} \State $(B^{(h)},M^{(h)},S^{(h)}) \gets$ \Call{NLopt}{$B^{(h-1)},M^{(h-1)},S^{(h-1)};\Omega^{(h)}$} \State $\Sigma^{(h)} \gets C^{-1}\left[{M^{(h)}}^\top M^{(h)} + \sum_c S_c^{(h)}\right]$ \State $F_h \gets -\mathrm{ELBO}(B^{(h)},M^{(h)},S^{(h)},\Omega^{(h)}) + \lambda\|\Omega^{(h)}\|_{1,W}$ \If{$|F_h-F_{h-1}|/|F_h| <$ ftol\_out} \State \textbf{break} \EndIf \EndFor \State \Return $(B,M,S,\Omega,\Sigma)$ \end{algorithmic} \end{algorithm}

Algorithm 4.1 is the block-coordinate scheme of Chiquet et al. (2018). The model, the variational bound and the role of the offset are in 3.2, PLN.

The latent moments (\boldsymbol{\mu}_{z},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}) are not on DeCovarT’s scale. Given the library s_c of a cell, the moments of its counts are

\mathbb{E}\,x_{gc}=s_{c}\,e^{\mu_{z,g}+\sigma_{z,gg}/2}, \qquad \operatorname{Cov}\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c} = \operatorname{diag}(\mathbb{E}\,\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c}) + \bigl(\mathbb{E}\,\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c}\,\mathbb{E}\,\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c}^{\top}\bigr) \circ \bigl(e^{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}}-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bigr), \tag{4.6}

with e^{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}} taken entrywise. On raw counts (normalise = "none"), pln_linear_moments(scale = "counts") averages Equation 4.6 over the libraries of the cells being scored, by the law of total covariance:

\bar{\boldsymbol{x}}=\bar s\,\boldsymbol{m}, \qquad \operatorname{Cov}\boldsymbol{x} = \bar s\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{m}) +\overline{s^{2}}\,(\boldsymbol{m}\boldsymbol{m}^{\top}) \circ\bigl(e^{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}}-\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}\bigr) +\operatorname{Var}(s)\,\boldsymbol{m}\boldsymbol{m}^{\top}, \qquad \boldsymbol{m}=e^{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{z}+\operatorname{diag}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z})/2}, \tag{4.7}

with \bar s, \overline{s^{2}} and \operatorname{Var}(s) the mean, mean square and variance of those libraries. This is the matrix that is scored, comparable with the raw sample covariance. The offset removes every size variation from \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{z}, biological size included, so PLNnetwork needs no moment correction, only a choice of what to put back at export. Beside the scored moments, the driver exports the two brackets without the Poisson diagonal (poisson_diag = FALSE): \kappa=0 keeps the depth terms (size_term = "keep"), and the all-technical end gives every cell the mean library, so \operatorname{Var}(s)=0 and \overline{s^{2}}=\bar s^{2} (size_term = "drop"). With normalise = "library" the moments are divided by the size factor instead (scale = "normalised").

4.3 Cross-validation and outputs

The same loss selects the tuning parameter of every arm and compares the five: the held-out Gaussian log-likelihood on the linear scale. For held-out cells \mathcal{T} and an estimate (\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}) fitted without them,

\ell(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}};\mathcal{T}) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{T}} \log\phi_{G}\bigl(\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot c};\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}\bigr), \tag{4.8}

computed by heldout_gaussian_logscore() through a Cholesky factor of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}; for the denoised arm, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}} is Equation 4.4. Why this score is a legitimate loss for the covariance even though cells are not Gaussian is in 3.2, Gaussian log-score, and why it replaces AIC, BIC and EBIC as the selector in 3.2, cross-validation.

\begin{algorithm} \caption{Tuning by K-fold cross-validation, and nested comparison of the five arms} \begin{algorithmic} \Procedure{CVSelect}{$X, f, \Lambda, K$} \State split the cells of $X$ into $K$ folds at random, with a fixed seed \For{$\lambda \in \Lambda$} \For{$k = 1,\ldots,K$} \State $(\hat\mu,\hat\Sigma) \gets f(X_{-k};\lambda)$ \State $\ell_k(\lambda) \gets$ \Call{HeldOutLogScore}{$\hat\mu,\hat\Sigma,X_k$} \EndFor \State $m(\lambda) \gets$ mean of $\ell_k(\lambda)$; $\mathrm{se}(\lambda) \gets$ its standard error \EndFor \State $\lambda_{\min} \gets \arg\max_{\lambda} m(\lambda)$ \State $\lambda_{1\mathrm{se}} \gets$ most regularised $\lambda$ with $m(\lambda) \ge m(\lambda_{\min}) - \mathrm{se}(\lambda_{\min})$ \State \Return $(m, \mathrm{se}, \lambda_{\min}, \lambda_{1\mathrm{se}})$ \EndProcedure \Procedure{NestedCompare}{$X, \{(f_e,\Lambda_e)\}_{e=1}^{5}, K_{\mathrm{out}}, K_{\mathrm{in}}$} \For{$k = 1,\ldots,K_{\mathrm{out}}$} \For{$e = 1,\ldots,5$} \State $\lambda_e \gets \lambda_{\min}$ of \Call{CVSelect}{$X_{-k}, f_e, \Lambda_e, K_{\mathrm{in}}$} \State $(\hat\mu,\hat\Sigma) \gets f_e(X_{-k};\lambda_e)$ \State $s_{ek} \gets$ \Call{HeldOutLogScore}{$\hat\mu,\hat\Sigma,X_k$} \EndFor \EndFor \State \Return $(s_{ek})$, paired by outer fold \EndProcedure \end{algorithmic} \end{algorithm}

Settings in scripts/03_04_cv_covariance.R:

  • cv_select_tuning(X, fit_fun, grid, K = 5, seed) returns the mean and standard error per grid value and the min and 1se choices. The exported fit uses min; 1se is reported beside it.
  • nested_cv_compare(X, estimators, K_outer = 5, K_inner = 5, seed) is run only to compare the five arms. The inner loop chooses the tuning parameter, the outer loop scores the chosen fit on cells it never saw. The denoised arm enters with \kappa=0; the \hat\kappa_{\max} variant is tuned by cv_select_tuning() alone (2).
  • Folds split cells of one unit. The score measures generalisation to new cells of this specimen, not to new specimens (Note 3.1).
  • In units with very few cells (29 cells for the somite differentiation front, against 5,821 panel genes) each training fold has about 23 cells; the shrinkage estimate is the only one expected to be stable there.
  • Run time. At 96 h the panel has 5,821 genes. SILGGM (D-S_GL) then tests about 1.7\times10^{7} gene pairs per unit and PLNnetwork updates a 5{,}821\times5{,}821 latent covariance at every step, each refitted K times for selection and K_{\mathrm{out}}(K_{\mathrm{in}}+1) times for the comparison; these two arms dominate the cost. No cap on the panel is imposed to save time.

Diagnostics that are computed but do not select. The held-out log-score of Equation 4.8 is the only selector. The diagnostics of 3.2, diagnostics describe a fit; none of them chooses a tuning value or an arm.

4.3.2 Evaluation

There is no ground-truth network for murine gastruloids, and the estimand is a covariance. The scores are therefore relative, in this order of weight:

  1. Held-out likelihood, outer folds of Algorithm 4.2: the selector, and the only score that compares all five arms on one scale.
  2. Downstream deconvolution: DeCovarT error on the matched 48 h bulk and in the misspecification sweep of Section 7.1, with \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} from each arm. This is the score tied to the paper’s estimand.
  3. Technical stability: a bootstrap of cells within type, labels fixed (Note 3.1). Edge-inclusion frequencies for the sparse estimators and the Frobenius spread of \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} for every arm. Conditional on this specimen.
  4. Separation of types: Hellinger and MixSim overlap of
  5. Concordance of the sparse estimators: overlap of edge sets (intersection over the smaller set) and Hamming distance between skeletons, raw and divided by G(G-1)/2. Agreement is a robustness number, not a validation.

4.4 Results and discussion

4.4.1 Regularisation shrinks covariance terms without setting them to zero

Status. The plug-in covariances of the first deconvolution pass exist (scripts/03_05_stats_plugin_covariance.R, outputs in output/grn/plugin/). They use the closed-form intensity \hat\lambda^{\ast}, floored at 10^{-3}, on the Welch-gated panel and on its subset with a deviance share R^2_D\ge 5\%. The cross-validation of Section 4.3 has not been run, so the intensity is not the cross-validated one that Section 4.2.1 announces.

What the estimator returns. With \boldsymbol{R}_j the sample correlation and \boldsymbol{D}_j the sample variances, the estimate is \boldsymbol{D}_j^{1/2}\{(1-\lambda)\boldsymbol{R}_j+\lambda\boldsymbol{I}\}\boldsymbol{D}_j^{1/2} (Section 4.2.1). Three readings follow.

  • Variances are untouched. Only the correlations move.
  • Every correlation is multiplied by 1-\lambda; none becomes zero. The estimate has as many non-zero off-diagonal terms as the sample covariance: all G_t(G_t-1)/2 pairs, that is 1.0 million at 48 h, 5.8 million at 72 h and 16.9 million at 96 h on the Welch-gated panel. Its inverse is dense as well, so no graph can be read from either matrix.
  • \lambda is a floor on the spectrum. Each eigenvalue \ell of \boldsymbol{R}_j becomes (1-\lambda)\ell+\lambda, so the smallest eigenvalue of the shrunk correlation is at least \lambda. That floor is what makes the estimate positive definite when C_j\le G_t.

Reading \hat\lambda^{\ast}. The closed-form intensity is the estimated share of the squared sample correlations that is sampling variance (Schäfer and Strimmer 2005). It rises when a unit has few cells for its number of genes (Table 4.6).

  • Abundant types are barely shrunk. In the three units with 3,589 cells or more, \hat\lambda^{\ast} is 0.006 to 0.03: the estimate is the sample correlation to within 3%, with all its sampling noise, and the floor on the correlation eigenvalues is only 0.006 to 0.03.
  • Rare types are shrunk towards independence. With 29 to 66 cells, \hat\lambda^{\ast} is 0.60 to 0.74: about 40% or less of each sample correlation is kept, and the estimate is close to a diagonal matrix.
  • One number for all pairs. The same factor applies to a pair of co-regulated genes and to a pair of unrelated genes. The estimator cannot keep one and remove the other.

A dense estimate is not evidence that every pair of genes interacts. Three mechanisms give a non-zero covariance to a pair with no direct link.

  • Sampling noise. A sample correlation is never exactly zero, even for independent genes. Shrinkage multiplies it by 1-\lambda; it does not threshold.
  • Covariance is not the network. Direct interactions are the zeros of the precision matrix. Two genes linked only through other genes still have a non-zero covariance. A covariance block is zero only between groups of genes with no path between them. Isolated groups can be looked for with the sparse arms (3, Section 4.2.4), not with this one.
  • Raw counts share a cell-size factor. The covariances are computed on raw counts (Section 4.1.1), so every pair of genes receives the size term \nu\,\mu_g\mu_h, positive for all pairs. This is the extrinsic noise that extrinsic_noise_nu() estimates (2).

Denoised arm. Removing the Poisson diagonal leaves a matrix with many negative eigenvalues, raised to zero before shrinkage (last column of Table 4.6). For the rarest types the count equals G_t-(C_j-1), the null space of the sample covariance (1,382 of 1,417 for the 36 naïve-pluripotency cells at 48 h): before shrinkage, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}^{\mathrm{bio}}_{j} has rank at most C_j-1, and \lambda alone makes it invertible.

What this implies for the deconvolution. The acceptance of a dense plug-in (Section 4.1) concerns the likelihood, not the interpretation. In the first pass the Newton failure rate did not differ between the four covariance inputs (22 to 28 of 48 fits each), so the level of regularisation is not what stops the optimiser. Its effect on the accuracy of \hat{\boldsymbol{p}} is large: on the Welch-gated panel at 96 h, the RMSE of DeCovarT on the raw covariance goes from 0.36 at \hat\lambda^{\ast} to 0.034 at a common \lambda=0.5 (Section 5.2.2.2). The closed-form intensity minimises the error of the covariance, not that of the composition.
