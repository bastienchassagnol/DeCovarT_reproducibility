3  One-versus-rest gene screen

3.1 Building \mathcal{G}_{0}

This chapter builds a working universe \mathcal{G}_{0} on which a GGM can be estimated. Each gene is scored marginally (one-versus-rest in spirit). That is cheap and biologically readable. It is the wrong place to stop for DeCovarT: concatenating per-type shortlists typically yields a multicollinear \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}} (Newman et al. 2015). The all-versus-all refinement that uses \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} comes after GRN inference (Section 7.1).

Notation follows the article Methods: gene g=1,\ldots,G; cell type j=1,\ldots,J; sample (here a cell) i=1,\ldots,n. The DeCovarT convolution is linear in \boldsymbol{p} only on an additive scale (Note 3.1).

Important 3.1: :warning: Keep the mixture on a linear scale

The convolution \boldsymbol{y}\mid(\boldsymbol{\zeta},\boldsymbol{p})\sim\mathcal{N}_{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p},\sum_{j}p_{j}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}) is linear in \boldsymbol{p} only if \boldsymbol{\mu} and \boldsymbol{y} live on the same additive scale (counts, CPM, or TPM). Logarithms, SCTransform Pearson residuals, and nonparanormal scores break that map (Sturm et al. 2019; Jin and Liu 2021; Hoffmann et al. 2006). Use residualisation only to rank genes; export DeCovarT moments on the linear scale. The probabilistic reason is Jensen’s inequality (Note 4.2, Note 4.1).

3.2 Highly variable genes as a signal-to-noise rank

Single-cell UMI tables mix depth noise with biological heterogeneity. Regularised negative-binomial regression in sctransform (Hafemeister et al. 2025) / Seurat::SCTransform separates the two via Pearson residuals (Hafemeister and Satija 2019). We use that transform only to rank genes, not to normalise the table that enters glasso or PLN, and not as a factorial arm of the GRN design (Section 4.2).

On this atlas the only technical covariate we actually have is library size. There is a single scRNA-seq resource, so batch, donor and unmeasured technical branches cannot be identified (Hafemeister and Satija 2019). The technical null is therefore

\log\mu_{ig} = \beta_{0g} + \operatorname{offset}(\log L_{i}), \qquad y_{ig}\sim\mathrm{NB}(\mu_{ig},\theta_{g}), \tag{3.1}

with L_{i} the library size of cell i. Do not put cell type j in this null: that would remove the between-type signal the panel is meant to keep.

Pearson residuals are

r_{ig} = \frac{y_{ig}-\hat{\mu}_{ig}} {\sqrt{\hat{\mu}_{ig}+\hat{\mu}_{ig}^{2}/\hat{\theta}_{g}}}. \tag{3.2}

Figure 3.2 recalls the NB mean–variance relation and the residual scale used by SCTransform.

3.2.1 Total, biological, and technical pieces

For gene g write V_{\mathrm{total},g}=\mathrm{Var}_{i}(r_{ig}) for residual variation after the depth null. That leftover is not pure biology: it still contains model misspecification. What we can split with the labels j is biological variation between versus within cell types,

\begin{aligned} \mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{between},g} &= \sum_{j=1}^{J} n_{j} \bigl(\bar{r}_{gj}-\bar{r}_{g}\bigr)^{2}, \qquad \bar{r}_{gj} = n_{j}^{-1}\sum_{i:j(i)=j}r_{ig},\\ \mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{within},g} &= \sum_{j=1}^{J} \sum_{i:j(i)=j} \bigl(r_{ig}-\bar{r}_{gj}\bigr)^{2}. \end{aligned} \tag{3.3}

Because the residuals are globally approximately centred, \bar{r}_{g}\approx 0 and \mathrm{SS}_{\mathrm{between},g}\approx\sum_{j}n_{j}\bar{r}_{gj}^{2}. A lineage marker (high between-type, low within-type) is the gene we want. A stress-response gene (low between-type, high within-type) is not, even if its total residual variance is large.

There is no calibrated differential-expression p-value here: one technical replicate cannot support DESeq2 (Note 3.3). The between-type sum of squares is a ranking score.

3.2.2 SCTransform versus FindVariableFeatures

Use Seurat::SCTransform() with Pearson residuals (residual.type = "pearson"; vst.flavor as in Figure 2.3). That is the residual transformation. Then do not stop at Seurat’s default variable features: those rank genes by overall residual variance, which mixes within-type heterogeneity into the score.

Do not use Seurat::FindVariableFeatures(method = "vst"). That path is log-normalisation plus the older variance-stabilising transform, not Pearson residuals of Equation 3.2.

After SCTransform, pull the residual matrix and compute Equation 3.3 by the labelled type j. Keep on the order of 2{,}000 genes in \mathcal{G}_{0} before any GGM (Friedman et al. 2008).

# Sketch: Pearson residuals for ranking only; export counts to the GGM.
sct <- Seurat::SCTransform(
  seu,
  method = "glmGamPoi",
  residual.feature = NULL,
  return.only.var.genes = FALSE,
  vst.flavor = "v2"
)
R <- Seurat::GetAssayData(sct, assay = "SCT", layer = "scale.data")
# rows gene g, columns cell i; align with cell-type factor j
j <- sct$celltype
ss_between <- apply(R, 1, function(r) {
  means <- tapply(r, j, mean)
  n_j <- tapply(r, j, length)
  sum(n_j * means^2)
})
ss_within <- apply(R, 1, function(r) {
  sum(tapply(r, j, function(z) sum((z - mean(z))^2)))
})
snr <- ss_between / pmax(ss_within, .Machine$double.eps)
Note 3.2: :information_source: HVGs are not a GGM input

Pearson residuals are closer to homoscedastic than raw UMI counts, which is why they are popular before PCA. They are still a nonlinear residualisation. Feeding them indiscriminately into SILGGM silently changes the estimand of \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} away from the latent Gaussian that DeCovarT wants at the population level. If a Gaussian GGM is used, prefer a copula / nonparanormal map on the same gene set (Section 4.3.1), or better a Poisson-lognormal latent Gaussian (Section 4.3.2).

3.3 Marker genes

Shannon entropy and Gini of relative means \pi_{gj}=\mu_{gj}/\sum_{\ell}\mu_{g\ell} are compaction tools for one-versus-rest lists, not all-versus-all selectors. Definitions, worked examples, and the warning that two markers of different types can still be collinear as rows of \boldsymbol{\mu} live in the DeCovarT vignette supp-S6-feature-selection.qmd.

On Suppinger the seed list is knowledge-driven, not DE-driven. The Excel signalling map data/cell markers/Mouse_gastruloid_signaling_network.xlsx was read with the Office Open XML tables (coloured cells mark a gene as a marker of that Excel type) and written as

data/dictionaries/mouse_gastruloid_signaling_markers.csv

with three columns:

  • cell_type_excel — lineage name in the spreadsheet;
  • cell_type_suppinger — one-to-one map onto a Suppinger annotation, or NA if that Excel type is not in the gastruloid atlas (extra-embryonic, cardiac mesoderm, haemato-endothelium, non-neural ectoderm);
  • gene_marker — gene symbol associated with that type.

These genes enter \mathcal{G}_{0} as seeds, not as the whole panel. They are the natural starting points for Markov-boundary expansion once a GGM exists (Section 3.6). Concatenating per-type seeds without an all-versus-all stage repeats Note 3.4.

3.4 Differential expression — only with biological replication

DESeq2, edgeR and limma–voom remain the workhorses of one-versus-rest ranking (Love et al. 2014). That logic does not transfer to GSE229513 as a DE experiment.

Warning 3.3: :warning: Technical pseudo-bulks are not biological replicates

Cells from a common experimental unit are not independent biological replicates. Splitting or bootstrapping cells characterises technical / sampling variability; it does not manufacture between-organoid uncertainty. Treating cells, or random partitions of cells, as DESeq2 replicates inflates apparent degrees of freedom and produces false discoveries (Squair et al. 2021; Crowell et al. 2020).

Use DESeq2 in this book only if independent biological replicates exist. For GSE229513, skip calibrated DE p-values. Rank genes by Equation 3.3 or by the knowledge table, not by a nested-model F treated as a p-value.

Bulk HTSeq tables (GSE229386) do have three biological replicates per time and Wnt schedule (Section 2.1). Those replicates support bulk DE of treatments, not cell-type GRNs from the scRNA-seq object.

Warning 3.4: :warning: Limitation of one-versus-rest concatenation

The union of per-type markers is usually a multicollinear \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathcal{G}} that is still too large for stable NNLS / GLS / DeCovarT Fisher information (Newman et al. 2015). A global all-versus-all stage is required (Section 7.1).

3.5 Differential networks as candidate generators

DeCovarT benefits from rewiring between populations. A gene that changes neighbourhood in \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} is a better candidate than a gene that is merely highly variable. Two strategies exist; both need graphs, so they nominate extras for \mathcal{G}_{0} after a first GGM pass, they do not replace the naive screen.

Fit every \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}, then compare supports. Independent glasso / SILGGM fits (Section 4.3.1) yield per-type edges; the symmetric difference of supports ranks genes by differential connectivity. The difference of two biased estimators is not a debiased estimator of \Delta_{j\ell}=\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\ell}.

Estimate \Delta_{j\ell} directly. D-trace methods target the difference of precisions without necessarily reconstructing both graphs first (Section 3.6). They cannot replace \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} in the generative model, but they are attractive feature objectives when the difference is sparse.

Either way, treat differential-network hits as candidates, not as the final panel.

3.6 Perspectives: INDEED and D-trace

These methods are not in the core Suppinger pipeline. Full detail lives in DeCovarT vignettes/supp-S6-feature-selection.qmd.

3.6.1 INDEED

INDEED multiplies a DE p-value by a permutation-filtered change in partial correlation (Zuo et al. 2016). On Suppinger the DE p-value assumes biological replication (Note 3.3), and the permutation null among cells of one gastruloid is a technical null. Use it, if at all, as a ranking heuristic with p_{g}^{\mathrm{DE}} replaced by a fold-change or between-type score.

3.6.2 D-trace

Zhang and Zou (Zhang and Zou 2014) introduced the lasso-penalised D-trace loss for a single sparse precision; the differential-network extension (Yuan et al. 2017) estimates \Delta_{j\ell}=\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}-\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\ell}. Empirically, D-trace is competitive when the difference is sparser than either graph (Plaksienko et al. 2025). It still does not produce a pair of SPD precisions. A workable proposal is: use D-trace to nominate rewiring genes, then estimate each \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} on that panel with a method from Section 4.4.

3.6.3 Markov boundaries

Once a GGM exists, seeds (markers, high-SNR HVGs, D-trace hits) can be grown by undirected neighbours \{g':\theta_{gg'}\neq 0\}. That is the Markov boundary in a Gaussian Markov random field, not DAG d-separation.

3.7 What we would actually run (naive stage)

  1. SCTransform Pearson residuals under Equation 3.1; rank by between/within Equation 3.3.
  2. Union with data/dictionaries/mouse_gastruloid_signaling_markers.csv (drop rows with cell_type_suppinger = NA unless a later atlas needs them).
  3. Cap |\mathcal{G}_{0}| at a few thousand genes.
  4. No DESeq2 p-values on technical sc pseudo-bulks.
