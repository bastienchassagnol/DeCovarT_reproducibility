4  Undirected GGMs from single-cell transcriptomics

DeCovarT needs one sparse Gaussian graphical model per cell type at the population (pseudo-bulk) level, not a per-cell precision and not a directed regulatory DAG. The estimand, aligned with the article, is

\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot j} \sim \mathcal{N}_{G}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}\bigr), \qquad \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} \equiv \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}^{-1} \ \text{sparse}, \tag{4.1}

with undirected edges encoding conditional dependence (Friedman et al. 2008; Mazumder and Hastie 2011). Single-cell UMI profiles are the only observations. They are not the objects that enter the deconvolution likelihood: we use them to estimate (\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}) for labelled types in the Suppinger murine gastruloid resource (Suppinger et al. 2023). Downstream, those parameters discriminate types via mean shifts and precision rewiring, then enter the convolution

\boldsymbol{y}_{\cdot i} \mid (\boldsymbol{\zeta},\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i}) \sim \mathcal{N}_{G}\!\bigl( \boldsymbol{\mu}\,\boldsymbol{p}_{\cdot i},\, \textstyle\sum_{j}p_{ji}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j} \bigr). \tag{4.2}

Some DeCovarT vignettes write \boldsymbol{\Omega}_{j} for the same precision; we keep \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} as in the article Methods.

4.1 Scope of the core pipeline

In scope: undirected GGMs; single-cell transcriptomics only; stationarity (no RNA velocity, no time-varying graphs); labelled cell types (Suppinger annotations), not unsupervised clustering of the graph.

Out of scope for the core, relocated to Section 4.6: multi-omics (ATAC, ChIP, spatial); causal DAGs; temporal dynamics; fused / joint graphs across types; copula constructions such as JGCGM that go beyond a Gaussian or Poisson-lognormal observation model.

Dataset constraint. GSE229513 is a 10x Chromium 3′ v3 UMI resource with one technical scRNA-seq experiment, not independent biological replicates for GRNs. GSE250136 is a later stem-embryo dataset; it is external validation, not extra Suppinger replicates. Technical bootstrap of cells is allowed (Section 5.5); biological bootstrap is not.

4.2 Observation processing is method-conditional

The first pipeline put SCTransform and huge::huge.npn() on the same Cartesian axis as the graph estimator. That is the wrong product. A transformation is part of the observation model of a given estimator, not an innocent preprocessing step that can be crossed with every GRN method.

Estimator family Observation Latent object for DeCovarT
SILGGM / glasso / sparse+low-rank GGM Continuous Gaussian (after NPN or, at most, as a sensitivity, Pearson residuals) \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} on that Gaussian scale
GmGM Gaussian copula / nonparanormal on the matrix gene-axis precision (and a cell-axis precision we do not export)
PLNmodels / PLNFGL Raw UMI counts precision of the latent log-normal Gaussian
Directed ZINB DAGs Counts with a structural-zero layer not a DeCovarT precision (Note 4.3)

If the method assumes a Gaussian vector, Gaussianise inside that method. If the method is a Poisson-lognormal hierarchical model, pass counts. Do not run SCTransform and then PLNnetwork: the PLN already has a Poisson sampling layer. Do not treat NPN as a global default (the improved pipeline is explicit that applying the ordinary nonparanormal map directly to counts can be problematic; JGCGM was written to avoid that).

The three huge.npn modes remain relevant only for the Gaussian branch (Liu et al. 2009; Zhao et al. 2022):

npn.func Returns Use with SILGGM
shrinkage n\times G inverse-normal scores Default Gaussianisation
truncation n\times G winsorised scores Heavy tails
skeptic G\times G correlation (rank copula; arXiv:1202.2169) Not a drop-in SILGGM data matrix

SCTransform Pearson residuals (Hafemeister and Satija 2019) stay in the HVG ranking of Section 3.2. Using them as the GGM table is a sensitivity arm, not a factorial factor.

Warning 4.1: :warning: Nonparanormal maps are not affine, so they break moments

huge::huge.npn() replaces each margin by a (truncated or shrunk) inverse-normal score (Liu et al. 2009). That map \varphi is monotone but not affine. Jensen’s inequality then says \varphi(\mathbb{E}[y_{g}])\neq\mathbb{E}[\varphi(y_{g})] unless the margin is degenerate (Note 4.2). A glasso fitted after NPN therefore targets the precision of a different random vector than the population Gaussian DeCovarT convolves. Use NPN only inside the SILGGM / Gaussian branch, never as a global preprocessing of counts, and never export NPN-scale means as \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j}.

Important 4.2: :warning: Transforming the response is usually the wrong GLM

DeCovarT’s mean map \boldsymbol{y}=\boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p} is additive on the measurement scale of \boldsymbol{y}. Replacing \boldsymbol{y} by \varphi(\boldsymbol{y}) and then treating the transform as Gaussian does three separate kinds of damage.

1. Jensen: moments of \varphi(\boldsymbol{y}) are not \varphi of the moments. For a convex \varphi, the probabilistic form of Jensen’s inequality states \varphi(\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{y}])\le\mathbb{E}[\varphi(\boldsymbol{y})], with equality iff \boldsymbol{y} is almost surely constant or \varphi is affine (Wikipedia, probabilistic form). The logarithm is concave, so

\mathbb{E}[\log y_{g}] \le \log\mathbb{E}[y_{g}], \tag{4.3}

and \log1p is likewise concave on [0,\infty). A glasso fitted to \log1p counts therefore targets a precision of a different random vector. The mean of the transformed table is not the transform of the mean that DeCovarT needs in Equation 4.2, and the covariance is not the delta-method image of \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j} unless one explicitly builds that expansion and lives with its bias. Population aggregation (Section 5.3) compounds the error: the sum of transformed cells is not the transform of the sum.

2. The estimand of a transformed linear model is not the estimand of the original mean. In a Gaussian linear model, \mathbb{E}[\log y\mid x]=\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}x describes multiplicative effects on y after exponentiation, not additive shifts of \mathbb{E}[y\mid x] (University of Virginia Library, interpreting log transformations). Coefficient recipes (e^{\hat\beta}-1 as a percent change, and the log–log elasticity case) are coherent only if that multiplicative model is the scientific target. DeCovarT’s target is a linear mixture of means plus a quadratic mixture of covariances. Percent changes on a log scale do not plug into \boldsymbol{\mu}\boldsymbol{p} or \sum_{j}p_{j}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}.

3. A GLM with the right mean–variance function is preferable to transforming y. Transforming the response to force Gaussianity, homoscedasticity, or linearity is a classical but brittle habit: it changes the parameter, complicates residuals, and still fails when the mean–variance relationship is mean-dependent (count overdispersion). The constructive alternative is a generalised linear (or hierarchical) model on the original y — here, Poisson-lognormal or negative-binomial sampling with a latent Gaussian — rather than log1p followed by glasso. That is the content of Olszewski’s argument against transforming the response in regression (LinkedIn article; activity permalink): test the mean–variance assumption; if it fails, change the model, not the measurement.

Consequence for this book. Variance-stabilising maps belong in HVG ranking and, when a method literally requires Gaussian rows, as a method-specific Gaussianisation. They are not a third axis of the benchmark. The improved pipeline’s factorial

\text{observation model} \times \text{precision estimator} \times \text{cross-type sharing}

collapses the first factor to: Gaussianised input if and only if the estimator is a Gaussian GGM; raw counts if the estimator is PLN. Cross-type sharing is appendix (Section 4.6).

4.3 Core comparators

Four families respect the core constraints (undirected, stationary, transcriptomics-only, exportable as a sparse SPD \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} or as a latent Gaussian precision). We compare them on assumptions versus Equation 4.1, not on unpublished accuracy numbers.

4.3.1 SILGGM: five inferential modes, one generative precision

SILGGM is a frequentist toolkit for high-dimensional GGMs (Zhang et al. 2018a, 2018b). After optional NPN, it takes an n\times G table and estimates \theta_{gg'} (or de-sparsified versions) with four inferential procedures, the last of which has two penalty flavours:

Mode What is computed Role
B_NW_SL Bivariate nodewise scaled lasso Edge-wise \hat\theta_{gg'}, CIs, p-values
D-S_NW_SL De-sparsified nodewise scaled lasso Debiased nodewise edges, CIs
D-S_GL De-sparsified graphical lasso Debiased glasso edges, CIs
GFC_SL GGM estimation with FDR, scaled lasso Simultaneous tests of all pairs
GFC_L Same with lasso (tuning for FDR) Preferred when n is small

All five share two steps: a lasso-type regularisation (graphical lasso or O(G) nodewise regressions), then (G^{2}-G)/2 asymptotically normal statistics under a sparsity condition on the maximum degree. global=TRUE extends the first three modes to the GFC FDR framework. Outputs include estimators, z-scores, p-values, and (for global mode) adjacency matrices at a target FDR.

What the first pipeline got wrong.

  1. D-S_GL is not universally preferred. The SILGGM simulations report that B_NW_SL and D-S_NW_SL can give better confidence-interval behaviour for non-zero partial correlations than D-S_GL. For a small gene set that needs CIs, B_NW_SL is the authors’ own recommendation; D-S_NW_SL is the cheaper alternative at G\sim 10^{4}. For whole-graph FDR, GFC_L is recommended when n is small (order 10^{2}) because tuning helps FDR; at larger n the four modes are similar.
  2. The de-sparsified formula is not 2\hat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}-S. The Janková–van de Geer estimator is \widehat{T}=2\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}\widehat{R}\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}} with \widehat{R} the empirical correlation (Jankova and Geer 2015). The first report’s 2\hat\Theta-S is incorrect.
  3. The debiased matrix is deliberately non-sparse. It is built for inference, not as a generative precision. Feeding \widehat{T} to DeCovarT would export a dense, not necessarily well-conditioned, \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}. The statistically clean composition (proposal) is: use debiasing / FDR to select a support, then re-estimate a positive-definite precision on that support (constrained MLE when the graph is chordal; otherwise a constrained glasso or a small ridge). That separates edge testing from the SPD object in Equation 4.1.

Pros. Fast C++ implementation; p-values and FDR; scales to thousands of genes; matches the undirected GGM language of DeCovarT; CRAN package.

Cons. Gaussian observations (NPN is a copula patch, not a count model); no dropout / library-size layer; independent cells; independent types (no pooling); debiased estimators are the wrong generative object; on UMI data the authors themselves used \log_{2}(\mathrm{UMI}+1) then NPN — which is exactly the transform Note 4.2 criticises. That said, Zhang and Zou (Zhang and Zou 2014) report that the graphical lasso estimator of a sparse precision is usually still support-consistent: it recovers the zeros of the true direct (conditional) interactions even when the multivariate Gaussian assumption does not hold. That is reassurance for edge screening, not a licence to treat the glasso point estimate as the DeCovarT generative \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}. We keep SILGGM as a benchmark branch, not as “the” pipeline, and we prefer GFC_L / glasso support plus an SPD refit over exporting D-S_GL point estimates.

It does not model unknown mixtures: subset cells by annotation first. A Bayesian latent mixture with dropout (BLGGM) is not in the 4\times 3\times 2 product: labels are already known, and MCMC plus a gene cap of a few hundred make it a perspective only.

4.3.2 Poisson-lognormal: Chiquet’s PLNmodels versus PLNFGL

Two PLN estimators share Aitchison and Ho’s hierarchical construction and differ in how they estimate \boldsymbol{\Theta}.

Observation model (common). For cell c of type j, library size S_{c}, latent intensities \boldsymbol{Z}_{c},

Y_{cg}\mid\boldsymbol{Z}_{c} \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(S_{c}Z_{cg}), \qquad \log\boldsymbol{Z}_{c} \sim \mathcal{N}_{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}), \quad \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}^{-1}. \tag{4.4}

Zeros are sampling zeros under a Poisson with random log-normal mean; a separate structural-zero (ZINB) layer is not required (Chiquet et al. 2021). That matches Svensson’s critique of automatic zero-inflation for droplet UMI data (Svensson 2020).

Chiquet: variational sparse PLN (PLNnetwork). The PLNmodels framework (Chiquet et al. 2025) treats the PLN as a versatile count GGM and estimates a sparse latent precision by variational inference with an \ell_{1} penalty on \boldsymbol{\Theta} (Chiquet et al. 2021, 2018). Later work in the same group studies zero-inflation inside the PLN family as an extension, not as the default (Batardière et al. 2025). The exported object is exactly a latent Gaussian precision — the DeCovarT estimand — with a sampling model that respects UMI counts. Fits are typically per condition unless one stacks a group penalty by hand.

Zhai et al. PLNFGL (2026). PLNFGL estimates a moment covariance for the latent Gaussian and plugs it into the fused graphical lasso of Danaher, Wang and Witten: within-type \ell_{1} sparsity plus a fusion penalty that shrinks \theta_{gg'}^{(j)} towards \theta_{gg'}^{(\ell)} across conditions (Zhai et al. 2026; Danaher et al. 2012). That is joint estimation. It belongs in Section 4.6 as a sharing strategy, not in the core independent-type comparison. It is nonetheless the closest published composition to “PLN observations + several related \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}”.

Chiquet PLNnetwork PLNFGL (Zhai)
Sampling PLN PLN
\Sigma Variational likelihood Moments + PD projection
\Theta \ell_{1} on one graph Fused glasso, J graphs
Sharing across types No (unless redesigned) Yes (\lambda_{2})
Core vs appendix Core Appendix

Pros of the PLN family. Count likelihood; latent Gaussian precision is the DeCovarT object; no obligatory NPN; library size S_{c} is explicit; fusion (PLNFGL) is available when we choose to pool.

Cons. Moment estimators of \Sigma can be crude in G\gg n; variational PLN is heavier than glasso; fusion can erase the rewiring DeCovarT wants if \lambda_{2} is large; none of these methods gives SILGGM-style edgewise FDR out of the box.

Critical recommendation. Cite Chiquet for the PLN model and variational sparse precision, and Zhai for moments + fusion. On Suppinger, the core PLN arm is Chiquet PLNnetwork per labelled type. PLNFGL is the fused extension.

4.3.3 Multi-axis GGMs (GmGM): cells are not independent rows

Almost every GGM above makes the independence assumption: to learn the gene precision, cells are i.i.d. rows. That is false in a gastruloid — cells share a microenvironment, a lineage, and (in spatial assays) neighbours. GmGM (Andrew et al. 2026) replaces independence with a Cartesian product assumption: gene g in cell c may depend on other genes in the same cell, or on the same gene in other cells, but not on arbitrary cell–gene pairs. The precision of \mathrm{vec}(X) is a Kronecker sum of a gene precision and a cell precision. A Gaussian copula (nonparanormal) weakens Gaussian margins. The 2026 contribution is scalability to millions of cells via a low-rank / linearly sparse eigendecomposition; the Python package is GmGM.

Pros. Direct stress test of the i.i.d.-cell assumption that underwrites both SILGGM and the aggregation algebra in Section 5.3; jointly estimates a cell graph (lineage / communication proxy) and a gene graph; copula margins.

Cons. The statistical object is not Equation 4.1: one gene precision is shared across cells, with residual dependence pushed into the cell axis. DeCovarT wants type-specific \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}, which requires subsetting types or interpreting the cell graph as a substitute for type labels. Kronecker structure is a modelling restriction. Multi-omics shared-axis examples in the paper (RNA+ATAC) are out of our core scope.

Use. External stress test, not the default DeCovarT estimator. If the gene-axis precision on a type subset disagrees violently with per-type SILGGM / PLN, the i.i.d. assumption is a suspect.

4.3.4 Sparse plus low-rank latent-variable GGMs

A sparse GGM assumes that, after conditioning on the observed genes, the remaining dependence is sparse. Unmeasured programmes — cell cycle, maturation, sequencing depth not captured by a size factor — induce a dense marginal precision. Chandrasekaran, Parrilo and Willsky separate a sparse conditional interaction matrix among observed genes from a low-rank contribution of latent factors (Chandrasekaran et al. 2012):

\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\mathrm{marg}} = \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\mathrm{sparse}} - \boldsymbol{L}, \qquad \boldsymbol{L}\succeq 0,\ \mathrm{rank}(\boldsymbol{L})\ll G. \tag{4.5}

Pros. Protects the gene graph against global confounders that are rampant in scRNA-seq; the sparse summand is the object one would like to call “regulation”; conceptually close to factor-adjusted GGMs.

Cons. Identifiability needs incoherence between the sparse and low-rank parts; tuning two penalties; the low-rank piece is not a DeCovarT type covariance — it is a nuisance. If the sparse component changes dramatically relative to ordinary glasso, inferred edges may have been global programmes.

Use. Robustness experiment on the same gene panel as SILGGM. If sparse edges are stable, ordinary glasso was not merely fitting cell cycle; if they collapse, do not export the dense glasso to DeCovarT.

4.4 Four methods to carry forward

Given stationarity, a multivariate Gaussian population object, sparsity, and single-cell transcriptomics only, the four most defensible core routes are:

  1. SILGGM GFC_L (or glasso) → FDR support → SPD refit, with NPN inside this branch only. Inferential, scalable, closest in language to the article’s gLasso precision (Mazumder and Hastie 2011; Zhang et al. 2018a).
  2. Chiquet PLNnetwork per labelled type. Count-honest latent Gaussian; no NPN; library size explicit (Chiquet et al. 2021).
  3. Sparse + low-rank GGM on the Gaussianised (or copula) scale. Confounder check before trusting \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}.
  4. GmGM on type subsets as a multi-axis stress test of independent cells — interpret the gene-axis precision, do not force the cell-axis precision into DeCovarT.

PLNFGL, joint graphical lasso, and JGCGM are sharing strategies (Section 4.6). They may well beat independent fits on variance, but they risk shrinking away the rewiring that is the scientific target. SCENIC / GRNBoost2 (Aibar et al. 2017; Moerman et al. 2019) are directed, TF-centred boosting methods: valuable as knowledge-driven baselines in Section 6.4, not as DeCovarT precisions.

Every core method must finally export the same object

\bigl\{ \widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j},\, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j},\, \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{j} \bigr\}_{j=1}^{J}, \qquad \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}_{j} = \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}^{-1} \succ 0. \tag{4.6}

Warning 4.3: :warning: Why we drop zero-inflated directed graphs in the core

Directed zero-inflated DAGs (for example arXiv:2512.16233) are the wrong object for DeCovarT: the output is a DAG, not an undirected SPD precision; projecting to a skeleton discards the Gaussian convolution that the likelihood needs.

There is a deeper sampling reason not to start from ZINB at all on this dataset. Suppinger is droplet UMI 10x data. Svensson showed that zeros in droplet scRNA-seq are often explained by ordinary molecule-sampling count distributions (Svensson 2020). Automatic zero-inflation is then unidentifiable and scientifically unmotivated.

Keep hurdle / ZINB graphs in the sensitivity chapter (Section 8.1) if we want to contaminate the observation model, not as a GRN estimator.

4.5 Revised design product

The improved pipeline’s three-way factorial is right in spirit and wrong in its first factor. The product we will actually interpret is (Figure 1)

\underbrace{\{\text{SILGGM+NPN},\ \text{PLN counts},\ \text{sparse+low-rank},\ \text{GmGM}\}}_{\text{four GGM families}} \times \underbrace{\{\text{affine},\ \text{Bures--Wasserstein},\ \text{scDesign2}\}}_{\text{three population lifts}} \times \underbrace{\{\mathcal{G}_{0},\ \text{optional NSGA-II}\}}_{\text{two panels}}. \tag{4.7}

That is up to 24 routes. Independent type-level fits are the core sharing strategy; fused / copula-joint sharing stays in Section 4.6. SCTransform and huge.npn are not crossed with PLN. BLGGM is not in the product.

Scripts (when implemented): scripts/03_01_grn_silggm.R and a PLN script still to be added. No code is given here.

4.6 Appendix: sharing and copulas

4.6.1 Fused and joint graphical lasso

Related gastruloid types should share part of their graph and differ on a sparse rewiring set. The joint graphical lasso (Danaher et al. 2012) estimates \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{J} together with a group or fused penalty. PLNFGL is the PLN observation layer plus fused glasso (Zhai et al. 2026).

The modelling decision — independent versus partially pooled precisions — is more consequential than glasso versus nodewise lasso. For DeCovarT the risk is shrinkage of \Delta_{j\ell}. If fusion is used, keep \lambda_{2} small and compare supports to the independent SILGGM / PLN fits. Full joint estimation is not the core default until we can show that rewiring of developmental neighbours is preserved.

4.6.2 Gaussian copulas beyond NPN: JGCGM

JGCGM learns several subgroup networks from scRNA-seq with a Gaussian copula and an explicit split between shared and subgroup-specific edges (Wu et al. 2020). The authors warn against naïve NPN on counts. That is the right warning; the method is still a copula GGM, hence undirected and stationary, but it is a joint estimator (appendix) and it does not provide a PLN sampling layer. Use it as a copula-aware fused comparator if an implementation is available; do not replace PLN on UMI counts without a benchmark.

Chiquet’s earlier work on latent organisation, sample heterogeneity, time-course, and multi-attribute GGMs (HDR; SIMoNe; cooperative lasso) supplies structured penalties (Chiquet et al. 2012) that could encode pathway modules. Those penalties are optional regularisers, not a second estimand.
