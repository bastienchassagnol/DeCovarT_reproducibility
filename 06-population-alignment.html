5  From single cells to type-level Gaussians

DeCovarT does not ingest single-cell profiles. It needs, for each labelled type j, a population-level Gaussian (\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}) whose precision is sparse. This chapter is about lifting cell-level observations to that object, and about checking that the lift still represents the cells.

5.1 Alignment of type-level Gaussians

Three strategies, in increasing ambition:

  1. Affine invariance / convolution — closed-form means and covariances when cells of one type are independent Gaussians.
  2. Optimal transport — maps and distances when we do not want to assume that the empirical cloud is already that Gaussian, including discrete (count) OT and Bures / Wasserstein-robust covariance.
  3. Resampling — technical bootstrap of cells, and Monte Carlo from mechanistic or copula simulators (scDesign2, scMultiSim).

5.2 Two aggregations that must not be confused

Write \boldsymbol{x}_{c\cdot j} for the expression vector of cell c in type j, and n_{j} for the number of such cells.

Type-level (population) pseudo-bulk — the object of this chapter —

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}_{\cdot j} = \sum_{c\in C_{j}} w_{c}\,\boldsymbol{x}_{c\cdot j}, \qquad \sum_{c}w_{c}=1 \ \text{or}\ w_{c}\equiv 1, \tag{5.1}

is a weighted sum within one type. If the cells are independent, means add and covariances add (with w_{c}^{2}). This is the aggregation that estimates \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j} and \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}.

Total bulk — the DeCovarT observation —

\boldsymbol{y} = \sum_{j=1}^{J} p_{j}\,\boldsymbol{x}_{\cdot j}^{\mathrm{pop}} \tag{5.2}

is a convolution across types, with simplex weights \boldsymbol{p}. Independence across types gives \mathrm{Cov}(\boldsymbol{y})=\sum_{j}p_{j}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}, which is the article’s likelihood. That p_{j}^{2} is not the same calculation as Equation 5.1.

The first pipeline described “sum the cells, \Sigma scales by n, zeros of \Theta are preserved” as if it applied to whatever one calls a pseudo-bulk. The improved pipeline corrected the algebra but still risked being read as a statement about total bulk \boldsymbol{y}. Here we only aggregate within type. Convolution across types is DeCovarT’s problem, not the GRN’s.

Important 5.1: :information_source: We align cells to type-level laws, not to \boldsymbol{y}

Alignment, Wasserstein distances, and bootstrap in this chapter compare

\{\boldsymbol{x}_{c\cdot j}\}_{c\in C_{j}} \quad\text{with}\quad \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}).

They do not transport single cells onto a mixed bulk, and they do not estimate \boldsymbol{p}. DecOT (Section 5.4.1) is a bulk deconvolution method that uses Wasserstein loss against a reference; that is a sibling of DeCovarT, not a way to build \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}.

5.3 Affine lift and the precision-sparsity caveat

5.3.1 Independent Gaussian cells

Assume, for a fixed type j (subscript dropped),

\boldsymbol{x}_{c} \sim \mathcal{N}_{G}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{c},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c}) \quad\text{independent in }c. \tag{5.3}

Affine closure of multivariate Gaussians (linear transformation theorem) gives

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}} = \sum_{c} w_{c}\boldsymbol{x}_{c} \sim \mathcal{N}_{G}\!\left( \sum_{c}w_{c}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{c},\; \sum_{c}w_{c}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c} \right). \tag{5.4}

Special cases used in practice:

Weights Mean Covariance Precision
w_{c}=1 (sum of n i.i.d. cells) n\boldsymbol{\mu} n\boldsymbol{\Sigma} n^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Theta}
w_{c}=1/n (average) \boldsymbol{\mu} n^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Sigma} n\boldsymbol{\Theta}
w_{c}=S_{c}/\sum S_{c'} (library-size weights) \sum w_{c}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{c} \sum w_{c}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c} inverse of that sum

The first pipeline stated that summing or averaging preserves the zero pattern of \boldsymbol{\Theta}. That is true only in the proportional-covariance case.

If \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c}=\boldsymbol{\Sigma} for all c, then \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}}=(\sum_{c}w_{c}^{2})\boldsymbol{\Sigma} and

\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}} = \frac{1}{\sum_{c}w_{c}^{2}}\,\boldsymbol{\Theta}, \tag{5.5}

so zeros are exactly preserved. If the cells have genuinely different \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c},

\boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}} = \Bigl(\sum_{c}w_{c}^{2}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c}\Bigr)^{-1}, \tag{5.6}

and inversion does not commute with summation. There is no general reason for the zero pattern of \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{x}}} to equal any individual cell’s precision, or the precision of the “typical” cell. Heterogeneous cells within a labelled type — a gastruloid “type” is already a continuum — therefore break naïve sparsity transfer.

What we do in the core. Treat labelled cells of type j as exchangeable draws from one \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}), estimate that law (GGM or PLN), and export it. The affine formulae then describe how a pseudo-bulk of i.i.d. draws from the same law would behave. They are not a licence to average heterogeneous \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{c} and keep the glasso zeros. If GmGM (Section 4.3.3) finds a non-trivial cell-axis precision, the i.i.d. hypothesis is already false and Equation 5.5 is an approximation.

5.3.2 Mean and covariance that DeCovarT actually wants

DeCovarT’s \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j} is a cross-cell mean of the purified profile, analogous to MuSiC’s cross-sample mean (Wang et al. 2019), not a single latent draw. Empirically,

\widehat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\cdot j} = \frac{1}{n_{j}}\sum_{c\in C_{j}}\boldsymbol{x}_{c\cdot j} \quad\text{(linear scale)}, \tag{5.7}

and \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j} comes from the GGM / PLN fit on the gene panel, not from the unstructured sample covariance of thousands of genes (that matrix is singular for G>n_{j} and dense). The glasso / PLN precision is inverted (or solved) to an SPD covariance for the likelihood. Scale conventions (n_{j} versus 1) must be fixed once: the convolution uses \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j} of the population profile, the same object whose mean is \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j}, not the covariance of the cell-average estimator.

5.4 Optimal transport

OT enters in three mathematically distinct roles. Only the second is central for exporting \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}.

5.4.1 Discrete OT between count histograms (DecOT)

Kantorovich OT between discrete measures \mu,\nu with ground cost M minimises \langle M,T\rangle over couplings with those marginals. Entropic regularisation W_{\gamma} makes the problem strongly convex and solvable by Sinkhorn (cours_spatial_omics.qmd; Cuturi 2013).

DecOT (Liu et al. 2022) uses that loss for bulk deconvolution: cell-type reference histograms C (averaged scRNA-seq) mix as CP\approx Y, and P is estimated by minimising \sum_{m}W_{\gamma}(Y_{m},CP_{\cdot m})_{M} with an entropic non-negativity regulariser. The ground cost M encodes gene–gene geometry (co-expression distances), so the loss is not elementwise \ell_{2} / KL. An ensemble over donor-specific references follows SCDC.

What survives under our constraints. DecOT is a competitor to DeCovarT’s proportion step, not a way to estimate \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}. Useful ideas to steal:

  • a ground cost on genes can be the same metric that a GGM already implies (e.g. M_{gg'}=1-|\hat\rho_{gg'}| on partial correlations) — but then OT and the GGM are no longer independent evidence;
  • entropic discrete OT is the right language if we align two count histograms (empirical mean cell versus type-level expected counts under PLN);
  • ensemble over donors does not apply (one resource).

Discrete OT on cells (transporting n_{j} cells onto n_{\ell} cells of another type) is a type-matching problem, not population GGM estimation. Semi-discrete OT (continuous source, discrete Laguerre / power cells; cours_spatial_omics.qmd) is the natural model when one side is a fitted Gaussian and the other is a finite cell cloud: the dual weights \boldsymbol{\varphi} inflate Voronoi cells until masses match. That is a diagnostic of which cells are surprising under \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}), not an estimator of \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j}.

5.4.2 Gaussian (Bures) Wasserstein — the right metric on DeCovarT objects

For two non-degenerate Gaussians P_{0}=\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{m}_{0},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{0}) and P_{1}=\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{m}_{1},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1}), the squared 2-Wasserstein distance with quadratic cost splits as

W_{2}^{2}(P_{0},P_{1}) = \|\boldsymbol{m}_{0}-\boldsymbol{m}_{1}\|_{2}^{2} + \operatorname{tr}\Bigl( \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1} -2 \bigl( \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{0}^{1/2} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{0}^{1/2} \bigr)^{1/2} \Bigr). \tag{5.8}

The second term is the Bures–Wasserstein metric on SPD covariances. The Monge map is affine (Brenier):

T(\boldsymbol{x}) = \boldsymbol{m}_{1} + A(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{m}_{0}), \qquad A = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1}^{1/2} \bigl( \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1}^{1/2} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{0} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1}^{1/2} \bigr)^{-1/2} \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{1}^{1/2}, \tag{5.9}

when the covariances are invertible. No discrete cell-to-cell OT is required merely to transport one fitted type-level Gaussian into another, or to transport a Gaussian approximation of the cell cloud onto the exported \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}).

Entropic OT between Gaussians remains closed form (Janati et al. 2020) and stays well behaved for degenerate covariances — relevant when G>n_{j} before a GGM projection.

Core use. W_{2} between (i) the empirical Gaussian of cells of type j (or a shrinkage covariance) and (ii) the exported GGM / PLN Gaussian is the alignment score of Section 6.2. Large Bures discrepancy with small mean discrepancy means the graph changed the covariance geometry; the opposite means we only moved the centroid.

5.4.3 Wasserstein-robust inverse covariance

Nguyen, Kuhn and Mohajerin Esfahani (Nguyen et al. 2018) estimate a precision by minimising a worst-case Stein loss over normal laws within a Wasserstein ball of the empirical normal \mathcal{N}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}). Without structural constraints the estimator is an analytical nonlinear shrinkage of the sample eigenvalues: invertible for G>n, rotation-equivariant, order-preserving. With conditional-independence constraints it becomes a sequential quadratic programme for a GGM.

That is more directly relevant to DeCovarT than OT-as-alignment theatre: it regularises \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j} on the SPD manifold that the likelihood uses. A composition (improved pipeline; proposal) is

\text{technical type-level covariances} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{Bures / Wasserstein shrinkage or barycentre} \;\longrightarrow\; \text{sparse precision projection}. \tag{5.10}

The barycentre of several bootstrap covariances in Bures geometry is a principled “average graph-aware covariance” when we do not want to average precisions (which does not commute with inversion either).

Adversarial alignment notes and scConfluence are not default aligners: they target multi-omics / embedding matching, which we excluded from the core. CARDAMOM-style OT is useful only insofar as the cost remains quadratic in the same gene space.

5.5 Bootstrap and Monte Carlo

5.5.1 Technical bootstrap (available)

Resample cells with replacement within type, labels fixed. Each replicate yields (\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}^{\ast},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}^{\ast}) and therefore:

  • edge-inclusion frequencies (Section 6.2);
  • a cloud of SPD covariances whose Bures barycentre is a candidate export;
  • Monte Carlo error for MixSim overlap and for DeCovarT \hat{\boldsymbol{p}} in downstream simulations.

This is uncertainty conditional on this gastruloid experiment. It does not stand in for organoid-to-organoid variability (Squair et al. 2021). Calling technical pseudo-bulks “biological replicates” is the error already flagged in Note 3.3.

5.5.2 Biological bootstrap (unavailable)

Multiple donors or organoids would allow resampling experimental units. Suppinger does not. External cohorts (e.g. GSE250136) are for validation, not for pooling into one \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} without a batch model.

5.5.3 Monte Carlo from generative models

When we need more type-level profiles than n_{j} cells, or a known skeleton for BEELINE-style AUPRC, we simulate.

scDesign2 (Sun et al. 2021) fits, per cell type, a copula: a count marginal (Poisson, NB, ZIP, or ZINB, chosen per gene) plus a Gaussian copula for dependence (Theorem 5.1). It preserves genes, captures correlations, and can vary cell number and depth. It is transparent (parameters are means, dispersions, copula correlations). For our purpose: fit the copula on the GGM / PLN skeleton (constrain or initialise the copula correlation from \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}), draw synthetic cells, re-aggregate, and measure how well \boldsymbol{\Theta}_{j} is recovered. Using ZINB marginals should be a sensitivity, not the default, for the same reason as Note 4.3.

Theorem 5.1 (Sklar’s theorem) Let F be a G-dimensional cdf with margins F_{1},\ldots,F_{G}. There exists a copula C:[0,1]^{G}\to[0,1] such that

F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{G}) = C\bigl(F_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,F_{G}(x_{G})\bigr). \tag{5.11}

If every F_{g} is continuous, C is unique. If some margins are discrete, C is uniquely determined only on \operatorname{ran}(F_{1})\times\cdots\times\operatorname{ran}(F_{G}), the Cartesian product of the ranges of the marginal cdfs (Wikipedia: Sklar’s theorem).

Note 5.2: :information_source: Discrete margins: uniqueness fails, so we jitter

The Gaussian copula takes C_{\Theta} to be the cdf of a G-dimensional standard normal with correlation (or precision) \boldsymbol{\Theta} after applying \Phi^{-1} to each uniform margin. That is the copula behind both huge.npn and scDesign2’s default dependence layer; the empirical copula is the rank analogue (Wikipedia: empirical copulas). Other parametric families (Clayton, Gumbel, t) change tail dependence; they are not required for the core simulator.

UMI counts y_{ig}\in\mathbb{N}_{0} are discrete, so Theorem 5.1 does not give a unique copula density. Two practical patches (Genest and Nešlehová, 2007, ASTIN Bulletin; not yet in bibliography/REFERENCES.bib, to add from Zotero before a review pass):

  1. Jittering / randomised quantile. Draw U_{ig}\sim\mathrm{Unif}(0,1) independent of the data and set y_{ig}^{\ast}=y_{ig}+U_{ig}-1 or y_{ig}^{\ast}=y_{ig}-U_{ig}, then apply the probability-integral transform to obtain approximately \mathrm{Unif}(0,1) margins on which a Gaussian copula is well defined (lecture notes).
  2. Mass-spreading copulas (bilinear / checkered extensions, Bernstein polynomial approximations) that spread the probability of each integer cell uniformly over a rectangle, without adding explicit jitter.

scDesign2 implements the copula on estimated count margins; treat jittering as the justification that a Gaussian copula on UMI tables is an approximation, not a uniqueness theorem.

scMultiSim (Li et al. 2025) is a mechanistic multi-omics simulator: GRN, chromatin, RNA velocity, spatial location, cell–cell interaction, technical noise. It can take a GRN as input and emit counts — the right tool for a known-skeleton benchmark in the spirit of BEELINE / SERGIO / dyngen. Most of its modalities (ATAC, space, velocity) are out of the core. The transcriptomic channel with a user GRN is in scope for metrics (Section 6.3). Do not fit scMultiSim’s full spatial/CCI stack merely to pad Suppinger.

What we reject as a default generator. Black-box GANs; simulators that do not preserve gene identity; using SERGIO’s random GRN as if it were gastruloid biology. The improved pipeline’s suggestion to feed the sparse GGM into scDesign2 / scMultiSim as a prior is the right benchmark design, not a way to invent biological replicates.

5.6 What we would actually run

  1. Default lift. Independent-type GGM or PLN \to export (\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\cdot j},\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j}) on the linear scale, with sparsity transfer trusted only as “one \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{j} per type”.
  2. Alignment diagnostic. Bures–Wasserstein and KL between that Gaussian and a shrinkage covariance of the cells; semi-discrete OT only if we need to flag outlier cells.
  3. Uncertainty. Technical bootstrap; Bures barycentre as an optional export; never labelled as biological replication.
  4. Simulation arm. scDesign2 copula constrained by \widehat{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}}_{j}; scMultiSim RNA channel when a known skeleton is required for AUPRC.
  5. Not in the core. DecOT as a DeCovarT replacement; scConfluence; unbalanced / spatial OT; averaging heterogeneous cell-level precisions and keeping the zeros.

The first pipeline’s affine story is kept as the i.i.d. special case. The improved pipeline’s sparsity caveat and Wasserstein-robust covariance are the parts that were missing. Both are now stated on type-level sums, not on total bulk \boldsymbol{y}.
